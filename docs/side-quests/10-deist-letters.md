## Quest title

What the Rocks Kept

## Existing-world touchpoint

Castle Deist. The castle is already on the world map (`castle-deist`, a dock, so the ship can land; the airship does not set down on this harbor). Its two floors stay as they are: Castle Deist (`castle-deist-1`) and Deist Keep (`castle-deist-2`). The quest starts on the rocks under those walls, at the waterline a fisher already uses, and never moves the keep, its chests, or anyone who stands inside.

## Premise

A fisher on the rocks below Castle Deist pulled a waxed bundle out of a crevice the tide refills. The pages are unsent letters from one household that kept a lean-to against the castle rock: Cira Voss, who mended straps and left a chair turned toward the water; her spouse Nedda, whose lance stood by that door and whose place at the table is empty in every letter; and Cira's sibling Edder, who went to sell lamp oil and was told to look on the household bench if he ever came back. Cira wrote. She did not send them. Nobody in the bundle is the last rider who still lives, and the pages do not say what became of the castle's riders, only that one chair was still waiting when the writing stopped.

The tone is elegy. The work is reading, then deciding whether the words should be carried to the bench, burned, or spoken once at the household's stone. There is no fight to finish the quest, no one to recruit, and no one to save.

## Why the player becomes involved

Ilan can sound the words out and will not. He lives on these rocks; if he reads the names aloud every morning, they become his weather. The party is already ashore at Castle Deist, strangers who will leave on a boat. He asks them to read the bundle and choose, because the choice should belong to someone who does not have to fish beside it afterward. He does not know a password, does not hire anyone, and does not offer news of the war. Walking away only delays him. The bundle stays in his creel until the party is willing to look.

## Key NPCs

- **Ilan**, fisher. The only new person. He works the rocks below Castle Deist, found the bundle, and abides by the choice. He does not join, does not fight, and does not follow the party off the island.
- **Cira, Nedda, and Edder Voss** are names on paper, not people on the map. They are not met, rescued, buried, or added to any room. Ilan will not help look for them, and he will not say whether they are alive.

No existing person is moved, rewritten, or required. The party does not get special lines. The figure already in Deist Keep is not Nedda, is not spoken to, and is not given these pages.

## New locations or spaces

One new space: the **shrine cove**, a single quiet screen down a wet stair from Ilan's rocks. It is not a new region on the world map and it does not connect to any cavern. A flat stone niche faces the water, with room left uncut where this household meant to set names and never did. It is their stone, not a temple, not a seal, and not a door. Nothing here attacks. Spray is the only hazard, and it does not reduce HP.

The rocks themselves are the existing approach under Castle Deist, not a second new place. Delivery, if chosen, uses a bench already inside Castle Deist on the lower floor.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** After the ship docks at Castle Deist, Ilan is on the rocks below the walls with the waxed bundle on a dry stone. He tells where he found it and asks the party to read. No password is required. The keep above can be ignored. If the party refuses, he keeps fishing and offers the bundle again later.

**Escalation.** The party reads three short letters in a page viewer, and may reread them until the choice is made. The first is Cira to Edder: the winter chair is still turned to the cove, and the soup went cold. The second is Cira about Nedda: the lance is still by the door; she will not invent a reason for the empty chair. The third is the outside wrap, addressed only "Edder, who knows the bench," asking that if the boat never goes, the pages be left where he was told to look, or else said once at the niche and not kept as a relic. Ilan then walks the party down to the shrine cove so the uncut stone can be seen beside what the letters name. Still no fight, and still no explanation of anything beyond this house.

**Climax.** On the rocks, with the cove already seen, Ilan asks what should be done. The party chooses one of three acts. A confirm line precedes it. The choice cannot be taken back, because the pages are either set down, burned, or spoken away.

**Resolution.** The chosen act is carried out, Ilan speaks one last line that matches it, and the quest ends. He remains a fisher. The cove keeps a small visible change and nothing else. Castle Deist continues as if these pages had never been a matter for the keep.

## Important player choices

There is one choice, made only after the three letters have been read and the shrine cove has been seen. It has three resolutions, written out again under Possible resolutions.

- **Deliver.** Carry the bundle up into Castle Deist and leave it on the household bench on the lower floor, the place the wrap names for Edder. No one is there to receive it. The party does not hunt Edder, and does not hand the pages to anyone in the keep.
- **Burn.** Put the pages into Ilan's cook-fire on the rocks. They are not copied into a journal first. When the fire is low, the ashes are tipped into the water.
- **Speak.** Return to the shrine cove and read the three letters aloud to the uncut niche, then weight the wet pages under the stone. Nothing answers. No word is learned. No one is called back.

Leaving before this choice is only a delay. It is not a fourth ending.

## Possible resolutions

**Deliver.** The bench on the lower floor of Castle Deist holds the closed bundle. Ilan says, "Then they are where the wrap asked, and not on my rocks." Edder does not appear. The niche in the shrine cove stays blank. The letters cannot be reread.

**Burn.** The fire takes the ink. Ilan does not thank the party for destroying them and does not scold them. He says, "The tide was going to have them. You were only sooner." The bench stays bare. The niche stays blank. Ash dulls the cook-stones for the rest of the game, a mark rather than a clue.

**Speak.** In the shrine cove the party reads Cira's words into the spray. No spirit, sign, or new speech comes back. The pages stay pinned under the niche, spoiled by water, too faint to take. Ilan, who would not read them himself, says only, "Someone said the names. I can live beside that." The bench stays bare.

Every resolution closes the quest. None of them finds the household, restores anyone, or opens a further errand.

## Gameplay opportunities

- A three-page reader on the bundle: advance, go back, and reread. Closing it does not consume the letters. The choice does.
- A short stair from the rocks into the shrine cove, one screen, with no encounter rate and no chest. The walk is the pacing, so the stone is seen before the decision.
- One irreversible choice with an explicit confirm, so a mis-press does not burn the quest.
- Delivery is a single interaction with a bench on the existing lower floor of Castle Deist. That interaction does not start a battle and does not open a chest.
- Burning is a short, unskippable-in-the-middle scene at Ilan's fire: three beats (offer, catch, ashes), then control returns. Speaking is the same reader, locked to "aloud," in the cove.
- Afterward the cove and the rocks show one persistent change matching the resolution (bundle absent and bench occupied; ash on the fire stones; pages under the niche). Ilan has a single epilogue line if talked to again.
- No new battle, no boss, no rank to grind, no password prompt, and no failure state. A wipe anywhere else in the game does not void the bundle. If the party has not chosen yet, Ilan still has it when they dock again.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Only what this household wrote down. Someone under Castle Deist mended straps, kept a lance by a domestic door, turned a chair toward a cove, and meant to cut names into a private niche when they were sure. A sibling who sold lamp oil was expected to know which bench was theirs. The letters stop before any of that is finished. They record an empty chair and a refusal to invent why it is empty.

They do not reveal why the castle's riders failed, who survives, or what the empire did or did not do here. They do not mention a cavern, an egg, a pendant, or a mother of anything that flies. They do not name, accuse, or mourn the person who later stands with the party and is lost in the Cyclone. Quoting the pages must not teach or require the words Dragoons or Wyverns. In the letters themselves, say "Nedda," "the lance by the door," and "the niche," and do not use those two words.

## Rewards

The choice is not a build choice. All three resolutions give the same thanks, so none of them is the profitable one: two Potions and one Hi-Potion from Ilan's boat box, and one free rest on the rocks that recovers the party the way an inn does and costs nothing. Existing items only. No new weapon, no accessory, no key item, no gil wage, and no tome.

What actually differs is the residue in Possible resolutions: where the pages end, Ilan's last line, and the look of the bench, the fire stones, or the niche. None of that residue is a mechanical bonus. Skipping the quest loses nothing the main road needs.

## Canon dependencies

The party needs a ship that can dock at Castle Deist. That is the whole gate. The quest does not read or set the story phase. It does not wait for the keep, the cavern, a joined ally, or anyone's death, and it does not change if those things have already happened. Its flags are local (`lettersRead`, and `lettersResolved` as deliver, burn, or speak).

It does not depend on the opening loss, Leon, Wild Rose, Scott, Minwu, Semitt Falls, Josef, the Goddess's Bell, Sunfire, the Dreadnought, Leila, the Leviathan, the Lamia, Hilda, the freeing of Fynn, the Cyclone, the Ultima Tome, Castle Palamecia, the Jade Passage, or either defeat of the Emperor. It does not depend on any other side quest.

## Collision risks

- **Deist Keep.** The dying man on `castle-deist-2`, his pendant, and the two words he teaches stay untouched. This quest never talks to him, never enters his dialogue, and never places the bundle on that floor. Nedda is a different person and is not in the room.
- **The last wyvern and the egg.** Deist Cavern, the nest, the egg, and the trust that hands it over are not mentioned and are not flagged. Finding, carrying, or ignoring the egg changes nothing here.
- **Ricard.** These are not his letters. He is not the author, the addressee, or the empty chair. The quest must not recruit him, kill him, save him, move him, or comment on him if he is in the party or already gone. Do not read `ricardJoined` or `ricardDead`.
- **Keywords.** Do not call the learn function for Dragoons or Wyverns, and do not check that the party already knows them. The other thirteen keywords are likewise unused.
- **Castle Deist floors and chests.** The cottage, the Phoenix Down, and the Stun tome stay where they are. The bench is a new interaction on the lower floor, not a replacement chest.
- **Paloom and the lamp oil.** Edder's trade is a line in a letter, not a visit. Do not add a stall, a shopkeeper line, or a trip to Paloom. Leila and the Paloom sailor are not involved.
- **Other additions.** This is not a courier errand, does not grant a Rebel Armband, does not use Heron Glade, and does not use or resemble an Alarm Clock.
- **Cast and terms.** Do not kill, revive, replace, or redefine Firion, Maria, Guy, Leon, Hilda, Minwu, Josef, Gordon, Leila, Ricard, Cid, Paul, Borghen, Scott, or the Emperor. Do not redefine Wild Rose, Mythril, Dreadnought, Airship, Sunfire, Goddess's Bell, Dragoons, Wyverns, Mysidia, Mask, Ekmet Teloess, Cyclone, Palamecia, Ultima Tome, or Jade Passage. The shrine cove is not the bell, not Kashuan, and not a Mysidian seal.
- **Names.** Ilan, Cira, Nedda, and Edder are new. If a later draft needs a different household name, keep the constraint that the writers are not the canon cast and are never put on the map.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

One fisher, one shrine cove, three pages, and one choice. It starts and ends on the rocks below Castle Deist. It adds no ally, no death, no password, and no step on the road from Fynn to the Cyclone and beyond. Ignoring it leaves Ilan fishing and the crevice empty of any further plot. Finishing it leaves a bench, a fire, or a niche slightly changed, and leaves every required fate exactly as it was.
