## Quest title

The Star Not in the Sky

## Existing-world touchpoint

The town of Mysidia. On the world map it is the dock town at (4, 27), entry map `mysidia`, kind `town`, `dock: true`. That tile is shoal (`h`), and so are (4, 26) and (4, 28). Island harbors are shoals: the airship will not set down, and the party's ship is what opens the town. The town itself is the shared open map, sixteen by twelve, with no encounters. Inn, shop, and priest already stand at their usual spots. The elder already stands at the hall spot (4, 6) and keeps the hall's business. This quest does not move him, does not add a world-map site, and does not put the party on the water. It uses the square, the unused second hall spot, and one stair up to a roof.

## Premise

Calder, a student of charts, logged a point of light the naked catalog does not contain. Through the roof lens it sat two fingers above the harbor piles, and only when the third pile read the lower groove. Maelin, the student who keeps the correction book, struck the tide clause. Her note says water is not a coordinate. She ruled the point a defect of the glass. Both of them are partly right, and neither has stood the three observations on the same visit. A nick in the lens folds in the glass buoy that marks the shoal. At that groove, and only then, the buoy appears inside the sighting frame. The coordinates are exact. The star is not in the sky.

## Why the player becomes involved

The party is already inside the town of Mysidia. Calder stops them in the square because they are not students. A witness who is not graded owes the preceptor nothing, and cannot be marked down for agreeing with him. He asks for one watch on the chart-house roof: write what the notch shows, what the lens shows, and what the tide rail shows. He will not take the page to the elder. Maelin has already told him the elder is busy with the hall and will not hear a dispute about a chart. The party may refuse. The rebellion does not require them to accept, and the town does not punish them if they walk on.

## Key NPCs

**Calder** — junior chart student. Exact about numbers, and poor at pretending the page does not matter. He begins in the square at (8, 4). Once the party accepts, he waits on the roof beside the chart and will not call the point true or false until the slip exists. He wants the measurement kept. He also wants the name of the point, if the sky will not have it, to stay his.

**Maelin** — student-preceptor, the senior student charged with the correction book. Dry, fair, and unwilling to let one instrument prove a claim. She stands at the second hall spot (10, 6), not at the elder's place. She files only a slip on which the notch, the lens, and the tide mark have all been written. She does not name the buoy for the party. She recognizes the conclusion when the three notes agree.

**Orrin** — a student copyist who stands at the roof rail. He reads tide marks aloud and then stops. He will say "Third pile. Lower groove." He will not say what the groove does to the chart. He is the witness with no theory to defend.

The elder is not a quest NPC. He remains the authority in the hall, busy, and is given no new function.

## New locations or spaces

One new space: the **chart-house roof**. A stair on a free floor tile of the town map (proposed at (12, 7)) climbs to it, and the roof's stair returns to that same tile. There is no attic, no second interior, and no new place on the world map.

The roof is a short open platform with no encounters, no boss, and no chest. From its rail the party sees the town's own piles and the glass buoy in the harbor they already docked in. The buoy is observed. It is never visited. Four talkable stations share the roof with Orrin: the chart board, the naked-eye notch, the chipped lens, and the rail over the tide marks.

The hatch stays shut until Calder is accepted. Maelin calls it a chart watch, not a thoroughfare. Refusing him leaves the hatch shut and the rest of town as it was.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** Calder asks in the square for a witness who is not graded. Refusal ends the conversation and changes nothing else; he will ask again on a later visit. Acceptance gives the disputed wording and opens the hatch. The wording is: south by east, two fingers above the pile line, seen in the lens, third pile at the lower groove. Maelin's strike runs through the groove clause. If asked, she states her rule and does not soften it: a claim needs two methods, and a single glass is a wish.

**Escalation.** On the roof, three stations disagree until they are read together. The order does not matter. The chart may be reread at any time; it is the measure, not a fourth proof.

- The notch, aimed on Calder's bearing, holds the five catalogued lights. No sixth light lies inside the frame. A bright speck sits just outside it, low and to the east of the cut, too low to be one of the five.
- The chipped lens, on the same aim, shows the five and a sixth inside the frame, two fingers above the piles. The nick is on the rim that faces the low speck. It folds that speck inward. It does not find a star.
- The rail looks down on the piles. The water stands on the lower groove of the third pile. Orrin names the mark and refuses the inference. The glass buoy rides just east of the sight-line, level with that groove. It is the speck.

Maelin had struck the only clause that makes the nick and the buoy meet. There is no day-night clock in this watch, and no tide to simulate. Until the slip is written, every look at the rail finds the lower groove. Leaving town does not fail the observations already made.

**Climax.** When the notch, the lens, and the tide have all been read, the chart board writes the slip, with Calder present and silent. The nick places the buoy two fingers above the piles. The logged point is that placement. Nothing in the catalog sits there. No enemy appears, and none is implied. The slip is the whole proof. Until it exists, Maelin will not take a hunch, and Calder will not give up the page.

**Resolution.** The party gives the finished slip either to Maelin or back to Calder. That gift is the choice below, and it is made only once. The roof stays open afterward. The stations repeat the finding. They do not offer another task. The hall is as busy as it was.

## Important player choices

The party may refuse Calder in the square. Refusal is not a resolution. The watch stays available, with no penalty and no flag the main journey can see.

After the slip is written, there is one choice: **file the correction with Maelin, or return the slip to Calder and leave the book unchanged.** It is carried out by speaking to one of them and giving the slip. Speaking to both beforehand only restates the ask. The first gift locks the quest. The two results are the two resolutions in the next section. The notes will not support a report that the point is a real star. Maelin rejects any claim the slip does not make.

## Possible resolutions

**Filed.** The party gives the slip to Maelin. She enters the correction in the book: method sound, object false, the sixth point struck, Calder's procedure kept in the margin. She pays the reading purse, 200 gil, and gives two Eye Drops, for the afterimage a nick leaves on the eye. Calder accepts the margin. He does not argue. He is precise about the credit and wistful about the name he lost. Maelin fits a plain hood on the lens so the nick cannot be logged as a discovery again. Orrin has nothing further to read.

**Unfiled.** The party gives the slip back to Calder. He reads it, agrees, and does not carry it down. The correction book still lists a star that is not in the sky. He pays the same 200 gil and two Eye Drops from his own kit, so the choice is not a better or a worse purse. He keeps the page. Maelin is not told. She does not punish anyone, and she does not reopen a case she does not know has been solved. Spoken to later, she still believes the point was a careless use of bad glass. The lens stays unhooded. No later scene corrects the book.

Neither resolution recruits, removes, or alters anyone in the party. The elder is not informed in either case.

## Gameplay opportunities

The activity is a non-combat observation puzzle. Each station uses the ordinary Talk action, in the same way a tablet or a practice post already speaks. There is no new battle, no flee check, no boss, and no growth trick.

Local flags, unread by story phase: the watch accepted; the notch seen; the lens seen; the tide seen; the slip ready; then exactly one of filed or unfiled. The slip becomes ready only when all three seen-flags are set. Talking to Maelin or Calder before that repeats the missing method, so the rule of two methods is taught by what they refuse to accept. No text parser is required. The player does not type the buoy's name. The third observation writes the conclusion.

The roof is a small map with encounter rate 0, one stair, four stations, and Orrin. The only edits to the town map are the stair, Calder in the square, and Maelin at the second hall spot. Those tiles are not the inn (3, 2), the shop (6, 2), the priest (9, 2), or the elder's hall tile (4, 6).

The resolving conversation pays the purse once. There is no chest to miss or to respawn. Eye Drops and gil already exist. The quest adds no spell, no weapon, and no accessory. Saving in town, leaving, and returning all keep partial observations. Nothing on the roof expires.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Students in the town of Mysidia keep a chart book apart from the hall. A point is entered only when two methods agree. One glass is not enough, and the naked eye alone is a refusal to check the glass. The senior student grades that book. The elder does not.

The harbor is a shoal. Painted grooves on the piles are how these students name the height of the water. A glass buoy marks the shallow a ship must not cut. From the roof, with the water at the lower groove, that buoy is bright enough to be written down as a low star if a nicked lens folds it into the frame. The error is local, and the craft that produced it is local. It does not give the town a new name, and it is not a term the player must learn before anyone will answer.

Why the elder will not hear this is left ordinary. A student's measurement sits beneath whatever business now fills the hall. The quest never says what that business is.

## Rewards

Both resolutions pay the same amount, so the record is the choice and the purse is not:

- 200 gil
- Eye Drops ×2

Filing also changes the correction book and hoods the lens. Returning the slip leaves the false point on the page and leaves the lens as Calder uses it. The hood is a prop on the mount, not an item in the pack. A chipped lens taken away would only invite a later use, and this quest stops when the slip is given.

Nothing here changes stats, spells, the shop list, the inn price of 80 gil, or the sanctuary. No unique equipment is awarded.

## Canon dependencies

None. The quest does not read or set a story phase. It does not require or teach any of the fifteen terms the campaign asks by name. It does not require a particular guest, a death, a rescue, or a dungeon. Being inside the town of Mysidia is the only condition. The ship rule that opens this shoal dock is the rule already used to enter the town. The quest does not check it, advance it, or change it.

Refusal and either resolution are invisible to the rebellion. No established name is redefined.

## Collision risks

Off limits for every beat above, and not to be added in implementation: the White Mask, the Black Mask, Ultima, the Ultima Tome, the Mysidian Tower seal, Minwu's death, the elder's mask errand, and the Cave of Mysidia mask chest. The roof looks at the town's piles. It is not a viewing platform for the cave or the tower, and the quest never enters either.

Further risks if the watch is built:

- Do not move the elder, and do not add lines on the words he already answers. Students never send the party to him. He never grades the chart. His plot function stays as it is.
- Do not teach a keyword from this quest, and do not make the town's name a password learned here. Student talk must not take the path that teaches terms. If the player asks a campaign term, the student has nothing to add and does not define it.
- Do not gate the stair on a story phase. Any party that can walk the town may be offered the watch, whether or not the hall has spoken to them.
- Do not put a boss, an encounter table, or a chest on the roof.
- Do not retune the shop, the inn, or the sanctuary list in the town of Mysidia. The world-map entry stays a dock town at (4, 27).
- Keep the new flags local. Phase checks, guest lists, and the lives of Firion, Maria, Guy, Leon, Hilda, Minwu, Josef, Gordon, Leila, Ricard, Cid, Paul, Borghen, Scott, and the Emperor do not read them.
- Do not require or produce any other side errand, its token, its trail, or its curative oddment.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

It starts in the square of the town of Mysidia and ends either at Maelin's book or in Calder's hands, both in that same town. The only new space is the chart-house roof. The only new people are Calder, Maelin, and Orrin. The buoy and the piles are the harbor the party already used. No other region is entered. No other quest's item or flag is required or written. Either resolution changes a student page and pays a small purse. It opens no road, no ship, no spell, no guest, and no later errand. Skipping it leaves the rebellion, the hall, and every term the campaign already teaches exactly as they were. The false point never has to be mentioned again.
