## Quest title

The Noon Stick

## Existing-world touchpoint

Salamand, the desert town on the world map at tile (33, 14), entry map `salamand`, kind `town`. That tile sits inside the desert stamp (x 30–36, y 12–16). The town interior is the existing encounter-free room: inn at (3, 2), shop at (6, 2), priest at (9, 2), hall at (4, 6), second hall at (10, 6), square at (8, 4), spawn at (2, 9), exit at (1, 10). The Salamand Inn still lets the living sleep for its listed 40 gil. Weapon, armor, magic, and item counters stay on their current stock.

The quest does not use those service tiles. Tomas, the recorder, stands in the south lane at (12, 8), clear of the square, both halls, and the exit. A stair at (13, 9) opens the seep. The hearing can be refused or ignored. Shops, the inn, the priest, and the road out keep working. Nothing in this quest reads or writes the campaign phase.

## Premise

Salamand does not drink the river. The water to the west is alkali: bitter in a cup, however useful a hull finds it. The town lives on a seep at its south edge, where fresh water climbs a crack in the limestone, strong at dawn and thin at noon when the heat draws it back.

Two houses both claim that mouth.

House Venn cut the high stone lip, the sand-trap of reed mats, and the shade awning. They keep a cedar measuring stick and read it at the hottest hour, when the flow is weakest and a lie is easiest to see. Sera Venn says a built mouth belongs to the builders, and that without their labor the seep silts shut. She has also been under-calling the noon mark, selling the "surplus" at dusk.

House Dorr watered flocks at the open crack before the lip existed. Maro Dorr says water that rises by itself is a town right, not a house right. His people have been cutting the reed mats, believing them a Venn fence. The mats are why the basin still runs clear. His niece Ilya was caught drawing before dawn. Pell Venn left a bruise on her wrist. He has stacked an unmortared private course that would turn the whole lip into Venn's yard.

A skin-seller, Ost, has made the shortage pay. A clay plug stamped with his seal sits in the lower crack, so Dorr's dawn draws fail and Venn looks like the only reliable mouth. The plug explains this month. It does not settle the older question of whose water it is. The quest is that question, answered with hands on the stone, not with a battle.

## Why the player becomes involved

Tomas will not ask either house to witness the other, and he will not call a soldier. A patrol's answer to a well is a lock, and the recorder wants the seep to stay a town thing. Strangers have no kin in Venn or Dorr, so he asks the party, already standing in the south lane, to hear both claims and then do the noon work themselves.

The party may refuse. Tomas steps aside. The campaign is not stalled, and no patrol arrives to punish the refusal. Saying yes does not enroll anyone in a faction and does not close the inn or the counters. The only pressure is civic: until the stick is read, both houses are blocking draws, buckets come up sandy, and the square's ordinary quiet is gone. Travelers feel that thirst the moment they want a full skin.

## Key NPCs

All of them are new, and all of them live only on the seep side of Salamand.

- **Tomas**, town recorder. Keeps the hearing book, offers the three hours (noon, dusk, before dawn), and calls the measuring when the party has spoken to both houses. He does not rule. He will not fight. After the work, he writes one line and closes the book.
- **Sera Venn**, elder of the cistern house. Proud, precise, and not a bandit. She paid for the lip and believes the stick is hers to read. She hid a hairline notch under her thumb, and she sold surplus skins to Ost. Confronted, she says the coin bought reed fiber after Dorr's hooks ruined the trap. That is only partly true. She will accept a public stick more easily than she will accept a broken lip.
- **Pell Venn**, Sera's nephew, a mason. He stacked the private course and he grabbed Ilya's wrist. He talks like a man ending a feud by finishing a wall. He is ashamed if made to say the grab aloud, and his loose stones are the material for a street trough if the party chooses that work.
- **Maro Dorr**, speaker for the kiln and the flocks. Hoarse, sunburnt, careful with his anger. His claim to the open crack is older than the lip. His reed-cutting is a real harm. He will not pretend otherwise once he is shown what the mats do. He has little coin and will say so.
- **Ilya Dorr**, Maro's niece, a young woman who runs the dawn draws. The bruise is visible. She is angry, not helpless. A potion offered to her is kindness, not a quest key. If the party never waits for dawn, she will still point at the plug when asked what stopped the crack.
- **Ost**, a skin-seller camped on the alkali edge of the basin. He is not a soldier and not a rebel. He sells dear water while the houses shout. His plug carries his seal. Shown the seal in public, he drops his skins and leaves. He never draws a weapon. He is a complication, not the owner of the dispute. Pulling the plug does not tell the party which house is right.

The man at the ordinary metal rack in town is not a witness, not a quest giver, and not spoken to here. He shoes hinges and beats bronze. Water law is not his fire.

## New locations or spaces

Three encounter-free spaces attached to Salamand. No random battles, no boss, no locked combat door. Heat is a line of text, not damage and not a status.

- **The Seep Basin** (`salamand-seep`). A limestone bowl south of the town room. North rim: the high lip, the reed sand-trap, the shade awning, and the cedar stick on its post. South rim: the lower crack and Ost's plug. East edge: Pell's unmortared course. West edge: alkali flat where Ost stands at dusk, still on this map, not on the world river and not on a road to another town. Stairs back to Salamand at the north entry. Inspect points, not loot chests: stick, lip, reeds, crack, plug, loose stones.
- **Venn Ledger** (`venn-ledger`). A dim room off the awning. Labor tallies for the lip and the mats are on the table. A second book, high on a shelf, records dusk sales to Ost. Sera will not offer that book. The party can ask for it after Maro mentions caravan skins, or simply read it if they look.
- **Dorr Kiln Yard** (`dorr-kiln`). Pots, thirsty animals, reed-hooks, and Ilya behind the drying racks. The place smells of clay and wet wool. It is a yard, not a cave. Leaving it returns to the basin.

No world-map location is added. The alkali edge is not a new travel node.

## Quest progression / major beats

### Beginning

The party enters Salamand by the ordinary door. Tomas is in the south lane. He states the shut seep, names both houses, and asks the party to hear them before he calls a measuring. He tells them the stick is read at noon, in the open, and that he wants kinless hands on the work.

Refusal ends the conversation. He remains in the lane and will ask once more on a later talk, then stop asking. Acceptance sends the party to Sera at the ledger and Maro at the kiln, in either order. Town services stay open the whole time. No guest is pulled in or sent away.

### Escalation

The party learns that both claims are true of different years, and that this month has been pinched.

Sera shows the labor tallies. The lip and the mats are real work. Maro shows cut reeds and the flocks. Ilya shows the bruise. Pell, at the loose stones, calls her a thief. If the party tells him to say the grab aloud in front of Tomas, he does, and later he carries stones without a speech. If they do not, he still carries stones when ordered, but he mutters.

Three hours, chosen by talking to Tomas, change what the basin shows. They are local waits, not a world clock.

- **Noon.** The flow is at its thinnest. Standing on the sun side of the stick shows a hairline extra notch that Sera's thumb covers from the shade. The public ration has been under-called.
- **Dusk.** Ost is on the alkali edge with skins for sale. The ledger's second book matches his prices. He will deny the plug until it is in someone's hand.
- **Before dawn.** The lower crack weeps around the clay. The plug comes free in the hand. His seal is in it. Ilya is there and will name the nights the crack was dead.

None of these visits is mandatory. After both houses have been heard, Tomas will call the measuring even if the plug is still in the stone and the hairline notch has not been seen. Missing evidence means a blinder choice, not a stalled quest. Ilya can still name the plug in one sentence if asked, so the dawn wait is the sure way to hold it, not the only way to know it exists.

There is no fight on the alkali, no chase, and no dungeon floor under the basin.

### Climax

Tomas calls both houses to the basin at noon. Buckets stay empty until the work is done. He asks the party to choose a physical act and perform it. The menu is four acts, always all four, offered as a button list in the same spirit as Travel. Speeches do not resolve the scene. Ost, if he has been seen, stands at the edge and does not enter the bowl. No battle starts. No status is inflicted. The sun is only the reason the hour matters.

The act is the choice named under Important player choices as the noon work. Its four results are repeated under Possible resolutions.

### Resolution

When the stones, the plug, and the stick are in their new places, Tomas reads the result into the hearing book and closes it. The houses drink or refuse to drink according to the act just performed. Ost leaves if his seal was shown; otherwise he slips off the alkali without a scene. Pell's grab is already either admitted or left as a private shame. The party receives only that act's reward, then is free to walk back through Salamand.

The quest does not repeat. Tomas, Sera, Maro, and Ilya keep a short stable line that matches the act. The seep map remains visitable and encounter-free, so the player can see the lip, the trough, the open pan, or the sealed crack as they left it. Campaign talk elsewhere in town is untouched. Leaving Salamand is the same road as before.

## Important player choices

Smaller choices change lines, not the water law.

- Offer Ilya a potion, or do not. She thanks the party or shrugs. If they have not waited for dawn, the gift is one way she trusts them enough to point at the plug. The plug can still be found without it.
- Make Pell admit the grab before the measuring, or leave it. Admission makes him quiet and useful during the work. Silence leaves the bruise as a fact the town already saw.
- Look at the sun-side notch, the dusk book, and the dawn plug, or call the measuring as soon as both houses have spoken. Tomas will say, in one forecast, what he thinks the chosen act will do to the flow. He will not block an act the party has not "earned."

The choice that changes the town is **the noon work**: what the party does to the seep with their hands when Tomas calls the hour. It always offers these four acts, and no others. Each act is a distinct resolution, written out again in the next section.

1. **Two mouths.** Pull the plug. Leave the high lip standing. Move the stick out of the awning's shade onto a public post and read the true noon mark aloud. Replant the reeds as town mats, not as a Venn fence. Venn draws from the lip at the posted hour. Dorr draws from the crack at dawn. Upkeep is shared labor, not a coin toll to Sera.
2. **One sluice.** Pull the plug, then seal the lower crack in front of both houses with town clay, witnessed, not hidden. Take Pell's loose stones down and build a street trough fed from the high lip. Tomas keeps the sluice pin. Add one trough-notch to the stick and read it in the sun.
3. **Venn's lip.** Give the stick back to Sera. Seal the lower crack as Venn maintenance. Let Pell's course stand. Dorr may buy draws or drive flocks onto the bitter flat.
4. **Open pan.** Lift the high-lip stones out with Dorr. Break the stick. Leave the crack unplugged and the sand-trap unrestored. Anyone may kneel and drink. No house reads a mark again.

## Possible resolutions

These are the four results of the noon work. Only one can be chosen. The quest ends when it is done.

### Two mouths

The lip stays, the plug is gone, and the stick is read in the sun where a thumb cannot hide a notch. Dawn belongs to Dorr's crack. Noon belongs to Venn's lip. Shared labor replaces the toll. Sera loses the right to skim a surplus and keeps the honor of knowing how the mats are laid. Maro loses the reed-hooks as a private tool and gains a dawn mouth that is no longer theft. Ilya draws in the open. Pell's private course is scattered back into the pile. If the seal was shown, Ost leaves his skins and goes. The basin stays clear on later visits. This is the most even law and the one that asks both houses to keep doing the part they hate.

### One sluice

The lower crack is closed in public, so there is nothing left to sneak and nothing left for a plug to fake. The high lip feeds a trough in the lane. Tomas holds the pin and reads the new notch at noon. Flocks drink slower than they did at an open crack, and Sera cannot sell a hidden surplus, because the trough is in the street. Pell's stones become the trough. He has to look at Ilya while he sets them. Ost has no dry month to sell into, and he goes. The water stays clean. The price of that cleanliness is a meter: nobody kneels in the basin anymore, and a lost pin would stop the town. Tomas is the pin's keeper, not a new lord, and the hearing book says both houses may demand a public reading if he fails to open the sluice at noon.

### Venn's lip

Sera keeps the stick, the lip, and a finished private course. The crack is sealed as her maintenance, whether or not the party ever learned whose plug it was. The basin runs clear, and the noon mark is still hers to call, hairline notch included if it was never exposed. If it was exposed, she abandons the false notch and still keeps the mouth; exposure changes her honesty, not her ownership. Dorr buys water or walks the flocks to the alkali. Maro shuts the kiln-yard door to the party. Ilya will not speak to them again. Some Dorr households camp on the bitter flat west of the basin. They do not move to another town, and they do not start a second errand. Ost loses his desperate buyers and drifts off without being accused, unless the seal was already shown, in which case he is simply gone. The town is quieter. It is also a town where one house owns thirst.

### Open pan

The lip comes out, the stick is broken, and the plug is discarded. Water sheets across the old pan. Anyone may drink, including strangers, and no toll survives the hour. Maro and Ilya treat the party as yard-kin. Sera shutters the ledger. Pell will not rebuild. On the next visit the noon water is cloudy, because the sand-trap is gone and nobody is paid to lay a new one. The inn still has beds, and the water tastes of silt. Ost remains on the flat for a while, selling "clean" skins to anyone who will not drink mud. He does not become a new quest. He is a sour note in a town that chose openness over a keeper. The seep is free, and it is worse.

## Gameplay opportunities

- **Talk, not keywords.** New NPC ids (`tomas`, `sera-venn`, `pell-venn`, `maro-dorr`, `ilya-dorr`, `ost`) use the existing conversation path. They never teach, require, or consume a campaign keyword. The player does not "ask" a password. They hear a house, then say they are ready.
- **A choice list at the climax.** Four buttons, the way Travel already lists destinations. Each button is one physical act and sets one flag. There is no combat command on this list.
- **Inspect the basin.** The stick, the hairline notch, the reeds, the crack, the plug, and the loose stones are interact points. The plug, once taken, is a quest item in the bag so it can be shown at noon. It is unsellable and useless outside this map.
- **Three local waits.** Tomas offers noon, dusk, and before dawn. Each wait only changes this map's visible evidence and which of Ost or Ilya is standing there. It does not advance story time, move other towns, or alter encounters on the desert.
- **A kindness with an existing item.** Handing over one potion softens Ilya and can reveal the plug early. Cure magic is not required. No status is applied to push the player into a cure.
- **Partial knowledge is playable.** The minimum path is Sera, Maro, Tomas, choose. The basin's puzzles are how the player learns what the act will cost. They are not locks.
- **A visible aftermath.** The same small map shows the remaining lip, the trough, the sealed crack, or the open muddy pan. No new mode. Walking back into it is the whole epilogue.
- **Growth is untouched.** No required swings, no spell drills, no formation trick. A player who never fights can finish the hearing.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Salamand's thirst is a seep, not the western river. People here will put a hull on bitter water and still refuse it in a cup. The civic clock is the hottest hour: the cedar stick is read when the climb is weakest, because that is when a false notch shows. A public reading, in the sun, is older than the empire's road and is the town's idea of fairness.

House Venn is a builder's claim. House Dorr is a user's claim. Both predate the occupation of the east, and both would rather shout in their own dust than hand the mouth to a quartermaster. The fear of a patrol is real in their talk. It is not a scheduled invasion. Ignoring the feud does not bring troops into Salamand, and settling it does not push troops out of anywhere else.

Scarcity has a shopkeeper. Ost did not create the two claims. He priced the gap between them, with a plug small enough to hide and a seal vain enough to find. Desert law, in this town, is stone, reed, and who may touch them at a named hour.

Ordinary metalwork goes on in the town the whole time: hinges, bronze, a rack of everyday arms. That craft does not sit in judgment on the seep, and this hearing does not ask it to. The kiln yard's clay is for pots and for an honest seal, not for a weapon.

What the party leaves behind is a rule the town can say aloud: two mouths with shared labor, one metered trough, a house that owns the lip, or an open pan that will silt. Salamand remains a desert town with an inn and counters. Only the south bowl changes.

## Rewards

No guest joins or leaves. No campaign keyword is learned. No stock is added to the weapon, armor, or magic counters. The base inn price in the town table stays 40 gil. Any discount is a separate flag checked only in Salamand.

The plug, if taken, is an unsellable quest item and is removed from the bag when the noon work ends.

- **Two mouths.** 150 gil from each house (300 gil). A clay witness-mark, unsellable. While the party carries it, a night at the Salamand Inn costs 20 gil instead of 40, and the item counter will give one seep-skin per visit. A seep-skin is a new consumable with the same heal-50 effect as a potion, not a new spell and not a piece of gear. Both houses will speak to the party afterward.
- **One sluice.** 300 gil from the old toll chest, paid by Tomas in public so it is not a theft. Three potions. The witness-mark discounts the Salamand Inn to 25 gil. No free seep-skin. The trough is the public good; the discount is smaller because the water is metered.
- **Venn's lip.** 400 gil from Sera, and one free night at the inn immediately. No witness-mark and no lasting discount. Dorr gives nothing and will not trade words. The clear basin is the whole of Venn's thanks beyond the coin.
- **Open pan.** No gil. Maro gives five potions and three seep-skins on the spot, which is what a poor yard can spare. No witness-mark. The inn stays at 40 gil, because the noon water is muddy and the house is not feeding the town's beds. Maro and Ilya remain friendly.

Rewards do not include a badge of allegiance, a clockwork trinket, a new trail, or anything that must be carried to another region.

## Canon dependencies

The quest needs only that Salamand can be entered and that its existing inn, shops, and priest keep their present jobs. It does not require the rebellion, the ferry, a guest, a vehicle, or any learned password. It does not read campaign phase and it does not set one.

It does not depend on any other side quest, shared flag, or shared item. Refusal, ignorance, and all four endings leave the main road through the town intact. No named campaign figure is moved, killed, restored, or replaced. No key term is given a new meaning. The fifteen passwords stay exactly what the campaign uses them for, and this hearing never speaks them.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

The hearing starts in Salamand's south lane and ends in the basin below it. Both houses, the recorder, and the skin-seller exist only for this dispute. No step asks the player to reach another world location, carry a token to a castle, or wait for a campaign chapter. The four outcomes change the bowl, the local lines, a few gil, some drinkable skins, and possibly the Salamand inn bill. They do not open a road, close a road, arm the party for a plot battle, or teach a password.

A player who never speaks to Tomas loses nothing the campaign promised them. A player who finishes any noon act can walk back past the same counters, on the same desert, and continue whatever they came to this town to do. Stripping the quest out means deleting these NPCs, three maps, the witness-mark check, and the `seep*` flags. Nothing else in the game looks for them.
