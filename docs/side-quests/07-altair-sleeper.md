## Quest title

The Third Cot

## Existing-world touchpoint

Altair, the town already on the world map, is the only place this quest touches. The party can already walk in after the opening loss. The innkeeper, the shop, the priest, Princess Hilda in the hall, the rebel in the square, the quartermaster, the practice post, and the study-shelf tomes all keep the tiles and the jobs they have now.

What the quest adds is a sheet door on the existing Altair floor, at (6, 9), south of the hall and clear of the square (8, 4), the practice post (12, 4), the shelf chests on x = 13, the quartermaster, and the town exit. The door opens a side room that has always been Altair's infirmary. It is not a new world-map location, and it does not move the canon wake off the town floor.

## Premise

Nessa Vell keeps three old cots in that room. Two are empty. On the third lies a man Ivo Brenn carried in from the reeds before dawn, shot in the back and still asleep from fever and blood loss. When he can talk, he says his name is Harl Doss, a levy private who walked away from his column. His own sergeant, Holk, shot him for it. Holk is not in Altair and is never fought.

Harl is an imperial deserter, and he is also useless. He was never told the rebellion's password. He was never shown the empire's flagship, has not seen it, and cannot describe it. He carried sacks. He knows a well in a levy village, the weight of a pack, and the name of the man who shot him. Pressing him for a word to trade, or for news of a ship, gets blank refusal. There is no coded hint under the refusal.

The quest is the choice of what to do with him once he is a person and not only a body. The tone stays moral, quiet, and uncomfortable. Nobody in the room is a villain for the position they take, and none of the endings feels clean.

## Why the player becomes involved

Nessa asks for hands, not for allegiance. She will not carry a living imperial into the hall, and she will not ask the square to decide for her. The priest's work is the sanctuary. The shop sells medicine and does not sit up at night. Ivo can lift a body and cannot watch the fever and the door at the same time.

The party is simply the people already in Altair who are neither the healer nor the patient. Nessa does not ask them to prove they belong to the rebellion, and she does not care whether they have spoken to the princess. They may refuse. The sheet stays where it is, and Harl is still on the cot whenever they come back.

Nothing on the objective line brings them here. The room does not expire if they leave to follow that line. There is no clock, in the story or in the mechanics, that kills him while the player is away. The night watch is a scene they start by sitting, not a deadline.

## Key NPCs

**Nessa Vell**, lay healer. New. She followed the town's wounded, not the rebellion's councils. She wants Harl treated as a patient until someone in this room chooses otherwise. She will not make that choice herself, and she will not walk him to the hall, the square, or the quartermaster. After the quest she still keeps the infirmary.

**Ivo Brenn**, orderly. New. He found Harl on the reed path, hid the torn coat, and will bolt the linen closet if the player tells him to. A rebel knot on his wrist does not make him the rebel in the square. He does not recruit, does not teach a password, and does not carry news out of the room.

**Harl Doss**, the sleeper. New. Levy private, deserter, wounded. He gives his name only when he wakes. He offers no intel because he has none. He never joins the party, never appears in another town, and is not a disguised canon character. Sergeant Holk is a name he says once. Holk has no sprite and no scene.

The rebel in the square, Princess Hilda, the quartermaster, the innkeeper, the shopkeeper, and the priest are not given new lines, new positions, or a role in this quest.

## New locations or spaces

**Altair Infirmary**, one small interior behind the sheet door. No encounters. Three cots, a stove, a washbasin, and Nessa's book. The player's way back is the same sheet, onto the Altair floor they already know.

**The linen closet**, a bolted alcove on that same map, not a second dungeon. It matters only if Harl is held. It has a cot, a bolt, and room for Ivo to sit.

**The shutter**, a crack onto the reeds at the back wall. It is not a town exit and not a world-map tile. Used for the release, it lets one unsteady man out. If the player tries it alone, the reeds go nowhere useful and the step returns them to the ward. Altair's real gate stays the gate at (1, 10).

Harl mentions Pellbridge, the village with the well. It is not a location. It is never added to travel, and it cannot be visited.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** The player goes through the sheet door at (6, 9). Nessa says a man bled on her linen before she knew the color of his coat. She asks them to sit with him or leave, and not to take this into the hall. Accepting sets the quest in motion and teaches nothing. Refusing postpones it with no penalty. Harl is asleep. A snapped belt plate, insignia scratched and not removed, is wrapped in the bandage. Nessa has already seen it.

**Escalation.** The player sits the watch. They can look at the wound, the plate, and the pack. The pack holds no papers, no map, and no sealed letter. In fever Harl talks about a well, about sacks, and about walking away. He does not say the rebel password. He does not say the flagship's name, or any shipwright's town, or any word that would shorten the war. Nessa tells them the choice will leave this room if they invite the hall in. She still does not go herself. The player may spend one of their own Potions, use Nessa's boiled linen and water, or do nothing. Lack of a Potion simply hides that option. Neglect does not skip the waking and does not fail the quest.

**Climax.** Harl wakes, long enough to drink and to be afraid. He gives his name. He asks not to be given up. He cannot buy mercy: asked for the rebellion's word, he says they never gave him one; asked about the great ship, he says ships are for people they trust with maps, and he carried sacks. The player then chooses what to do with him. That choice is the quest. No battle precedes it, and no battle follows it.

**Resolution.** The room enacts the choice and then stops. Nessa writes one line in her book. Ivo either latches the shutter, bolts the closet, or strips the cot. The Altair objective line is not replaced. The square, the hall, and the quartermaster behave as they did before the sheet was opened. Coming back later shows only the local aftermath below.

## Important player choices

The only choice that changes the ending is what to do with Harl once he is awake and has admitted he knows nothing worth trading.

- **Shelter him, then let him go.** Hide him through the rest of the scene. At the shutter, let him leave Altair on foot. He does not join anyone. He takes no errand.
- **Hand him to the rebels in this room.** Tell Ivo to bolt him in the linen closet and feed him. The "rebels" are Ivo's watch, not the square, not Hilda, and not the quartermaster. Harl stays a local prisoner.
- **Stop care.** Tell Nessa the cot is for someone else and do not sit the rest of the night. She leaves the room. The fever finishes what Holk started. There is no strike, no execution scene, and no battle.

Smaller acts during the watch — a Potion, her linen and water, or nothing; looking at the plate; trying to ask him things he cannot know — change her wording and what remains in the pack. They do not open extra endings, and they do not lock any of the three.

The player may also refuse the quest at the door, or walk out before the climax. That is delay, not a resolution. Harl is still on the cot later.

## Possible resolutions

**Released.** Harl goes out by the shutter before the scene ends. The shutter is latched again. Nessa says that if he lives, he lives where this room will not hear of it. No later town reports him. No imperial patrol arrives because he was freed. No rebel scold arrives because he was hidden. The cot is empty on later visits. Nessa returns to ordinary infirmary talk. He left the mended civilian cloak, described under Rewards. His belt plate stays in the stove.

**Held.** Ivo bolts the closet and says not to wait for the war to notice. Harl is fed and kept in Altair. He is not transferred to Fynn, to a campaign dungeon, or to anyone's party. He is not interrogated again, because the first talk already proved he has nothing. On later visits he is alive, sweeping, and ashamed. The wider war is unchanged: holding him does not weaken the empire, does not protect Altair in any later battle, and does not alter who lives or who wins. Ivo offers the leftover field linen, not a bounty.

**Died of the wound.** By morning the cot is empty and washed. Nessa has written "fever." She will say the book has no column for what the player decided. She does not forgive it in a speech, and she does not withhold the door. Harl is not raised. He was never a party member, the priest is not called, and a Phoenix Down has nothing to catch. Later visits get the same short fact: fever, an empty cot, Nessa still working. There is no item reward on this resolution, and that emptiness is deliberate. The quest is still complete.

All three are reachable without combat and without any keyword. All three leave the campaign's outcome alone.

## Gameplay opportunities

The activity is attendance and a decision, using the talk and examine flow the town already has. The infirmary's encounter rate stays at zero. The quest never calls a battle, never uses the practice post, and never puts a hostile NPC on the cot.

The watch is a short chain of examinations: breath, bandage, scratched plate, empty pack. Optional care spends either one Potion the player already owns or the room's own linen and water. Nessa's supplies are not the shop inventory and do not have to be bought. Care is never a stat check and never required to reach the choice.

If the player uses a learned keyword on Harl, Nessa, or Ivo, the answer is that this room has nothing to say about it. No keyword is taught. Cure, if the player already knows it and chooses to cast it, spends MP under the existing magic rules and is not required, not taught here, and not a substitute for the choice.

The room should allow saving, as Altair the town already does. It must not be treated as a dungeon with encounters. The closet and the shutter are the same small map, not extra floors.

After the resolution, the infirmary remains a quiet place to re-read one aftermath line. It does not become a shop, a sanctuary, a spell shelf, or a training dummy.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Altair's infirmary is older than the rebellion's use of the town. The cots were for fever and mill injuries before they were for a war. Nessa's book still has those older lines in it.

The empire levies farm villages and tells those soldiers almost nothing. Harl knows his sergeant and his sacks. He does not know the password that would let him live in this town, and he does not know what the shipwrights are building. That ignorance is how he was spent. It is not a riddle.

Deserters are shot by their own column. His wound is in the back. A man sent here as a spy would have arrived with a story that helped someone. He arrived with a hole and a plate he failed to scratch clean.

This room has no prisoner rule because Nessa has refused to ask the hall for one. That is a gap in one infirmary, not a new law for the rebellion, and not a doctrine later scenes have to honor.

Pellbridge is a well and a sister's name. It stays off the map so the player cannot turn his past into a destination.

## Rewards

Rewards are small, local, and different on purpose. None of them is gil paid by the quartermaster, a dispatch, a medal, a password, a key item, or a sleep cure.

**Released.** Harl's **Mended Cloak**, an accessory with 1 defense and 1 evasion, unsellable, priced at nothing. It is civilian cloth. It is not a uniform and not a rebel badge. It will not matter next to real armor. It is a keepsake the player may equip or ignore.

**Held.** Two **Field Linen** consumables, each restoring about 40 HP, absent from every shop, with no status effect. They are leftover bandages from a closet that no longer needs both rolls. They do not cure sleep, poison, or any other ailment, and they are not Potions.

**Died of the wound.** Nothing. Medicine the player chose not to spend stays in the pack under ordinary item rules. The belt plate is not loot. Nessa does not pay them for an empty cot.

No path grants a spell, a tome, a weapon, gil, or a guest. No path raises stats except whatever the existing rules already do if the player voluntarily casts magic they already know.

## Canon dependencies

The quest needs only Altair, enterable once the player has control there after the opening loss. It does not read or set the story phase. It does not require, teach, or consume any of the fifteen keywords. It does not require the player to have joined the rebellion or to have refused it.

It does not depend on the Gatrea errand, the practice post, the study shelf, the Alarm Clock, or any other side quest. Nothing in the campaign depends on it. It may be finished in one visit, left until any later return to Altair, or ignored through the ending.

It does not use Princess Hilda's recruitment, Scott, the ring, Minwu's joining, or Leon. It does not mention them. Harl's presence does not move the opening loss, the missing brother, the flagship, or any later canon beat.

## Collision risks

The sheet door has to stay off occupied tiles: inn (3, 2), shop (6, 2), priest (9, 2), Hilda's hall (4, 6), the square rebel (8, 4), the quartermaster (10, 6), the practice post (12, 4), the shelf chests at x = 13, y = 2 through 8, and the town exit (1, 10). Those scripts stay as they are. The infirmary must not become a second place to learn status magic or to hit a dummy.

Harl, Nessa, and Ivo must not teach the rebel password and must not require it. Harl must not know it. He also must not know the flagship: no rumor, no "great ship to the east," no pass, no cave, no metal. A player who came here hoping to skip the war learns nothing useful.

Handing him over must set only local infirmary state. It must not change phase, flags for the rebellion, the Dreadnought, Fynn, or the ending. Ivo is not a new square rebel and not a courier. The quartermaster must not receive Harl, a letter, or a payment. Do not echo the Gatrea errand's shape: no sealed dispatch, no 150 gil, no armband.

Do not add a second Alarm Clock, and do not make this quest use the existing one. Harl's sleep is a wound and a fever, not the Sleep status. A hit does not wake him. Field Linen must not cure sleep.

Do not send the player out the shutter onto the world map. That would be a second Altair exit. Do not put Pellbridge on the travel list.

Saving inside the room has to keep working. A new map id that the town-save list does not know would trap the player unable to save until they stepped back onto the Altair floor. The interior belongs to Altair for that purpose, with no random encounters.

Do not retcon the canon wake into this room. The party still comes to on the town floor they come to on now. Nessa may have heard that strangers were tended in Altair. She did not host that scene.

Names, deaths, and returns of canon people stay untouched. The priest's raise service is not extended to Harl. No guest is added or removed.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

Everything that can change is inside one room in Altair: three new people, a closet, a shutter, and a single decision about a wounded man who knows nothing the war can use. The quest has no prerequisite beside being able to walk into Altair, and it unlocks nothing else. Ignoring it leaves every existing Altair job in place.

Releasing Harl does not punish or protect the town later. Holding him is a local fact about a closet, and the war's outcome does not move. Letting him die fills one line in Nessa's book and no other record. In every case the objective line, the password, the hall, the square, and the campaign proceed as they do when this door is never opened.
