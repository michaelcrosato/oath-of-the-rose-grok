## Quest title

The Shoal Glass

## Existing-world touchpoint

Tropical Island, on the Tropical Shore (`tropical-1`), not in the Pirate Camp (`tropical-2`).

The island is already a dock at world (23, 27). Island harbors are shoals, so the airship still will not set down here. The party reaches the shore only by ship, the same rule that opens this island in the main game. The existing chain from Tropical Shore to Pirate Camp, the Black Mask chest on that camp floor, and the scripted pirate fight stay on the other side of the rocks. This quest adds a side stair off the shore that does not pass through the camp and does not become the next floor in that chain.

Leila's Sail command still drops the party straight onto the Pirate Camp and into her crew. The Shoal Glass never starts from Sail.

## Premise

On the far side of Tropical Island, away from the cove the pirates used, storm-fused sand has worn into panes of sea-glass in the tide pools. The panes hold voices. Not ghosts that can be fought, and not messages from the war: a drowned fisher finishing a depth-call, another arguing with gulls about a net, and terns repeating a cry a moment after the sky has gone quiet. The glass is warm on the face that turned toward the sea and cold on the face that turned toward the camp. It shows no one. The voice comes out of the thickness.

Pemma, a naturalist from an unnamed southern shoal, has been lifting the panes onto a dry ledge before the tide can roll them together. When two panes touch, the fisher and the birds interrupt each other. She wants extra hands, and then she wants a decision, because she can hear that the ledge is becoming a chorus.

The work is listening, and then choosing what the glass is for. Nothing on the ledge has to be killed.

## Why the player becomes involved

Once the party has a ship of their own, they can dock at Tropical Island and walk the Tropical Shore. A glint and a line of wet footprints lead away from the path into the Pirate Camp. The footprints end at a woman waist-deep in a pool, holding a pane up with both wrists because she will not pinch it hard enough to cloud it.

She asks for help lifting, not for allegiance. The party can refuse and leave. The main road of the island does not close. If they stay, it is because the pane answers a step they just took: the water repeats the footfall a heartbeat late. Curiosity is the whole hook. No one in Fynn, Altair, or Paloom sends them, and no password is required.

## Key NPCs

**Pemma.** An original naturalist. She is not a mage, not a pirate, and not from Paloom, Poft, Mysidia, or Deist. She writes what the pools do in a salt-stained notebook that is not a keyword list and not a tome. She is practical, a little frightened, and unwilling to drop a pane once it has started speaking. She survives every resolution. She either remains on the ledge, keeps the archive, or leaves the island on her own feet. She is never a guest and never joins the party.

**The green-net fisher.** A voice only. He drowned when a shoal shifted under a small boat. He repeats a depth he was wrong about, then the correction he did not live to use. He has no name the party can carry into town, and he is not any member of the cast.

**The tern-counter.** A voice only. A local woman who counted birds before a crossing she did not finish. She speaks of knots, headings, and the color of the water. She is not a princess, not a rebel, and not kin to anyone in the party.

**The terns.** A stacked bird-voice in a third pane. They answer late. If Guy is present and alive he can say they are speaking bird, then stop, because the air over the pool is empty. If he is absent or dead, the party simply hears the delay. The quest does not require him.

No other named person appears. Leila, if she is in the party or the reserve, has no lines here and learns nothing about her crew. The glass does not speak with the Emperor, Minwu, Ricard, Scott, Leon, Josef, Hilda, or anyone else from the war.

## New locations or spaces

**The Listening Pools** (`tropical-pools`), one quiet map attached sideways to Tropical Shore.

It is a short rock shelf and three basins, not a second dungeon. Encounter rate is 0. There is no boss tile, no chest that holds a plot key, and no stair except the one back to `tropical-1`. The existing stair from `tropical-1` to `tropical-2` is not moved.

Spaces on the map:

- **The Lifting Shelf.** Dry rock where Pemma sets panes. This is where the choice is made.
- **The Green Net Pool.** The fisher's pane, still seated in the rock until it is lifted.
- **The Tern Basin.** Two panes close enough to chorus if they are carried to the shelf and allowed to touch: the tern-counter and the birds.

The Pirate Camp remains visible only as a direction the party is not taken. Pemma will not walk that way. She says the cold face of the glass points there, and she does not want the panes stacked where people shout.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** The party docks at Tropical Island by ship and enters the Tropical Shore. Following the glint, not the camp path, puts them in the Listening Pools. Pemma is lifting the first pane and asks them to take the other edge. Agreeing starts the quest. Refusing leaves her there; the stair back still works, and she will ask again on a later visit. Nothing is removed from the shore, and the eye-drops chest on `tropical-1` is not this quest.

**Escalation.** Three panes have to come up, one at a time. Each lift is a short choice of which edge to support. A bad edge clouds the pane. It clears if the party sets it back in its basin and tries again. There is no fail state that traps them on the island. Before a pane will lie quiet on the shelf, someone must listen. Listening is a talk choice, not a keyword. The fisher gives a wrong depth and then the right one. The tern-counter names a heading that is only useful to a boat already among these rocks. The birds answer after a silence. If the party tries any of the fifteen keywords on Pemma or on a pane, the glass goes dull and does not repeat the word. Pemma says she does not know it. No keyword is learned, and no story flag outside this quest changes.

The uncanny turn sits in the third listening. The pane uses an ordinary word someone in the party just said — water, hand, cold — and finishes a sentence the dead were already saying. It cannot use a name from the war. If two lifted panes touch early, they talk over each other until separated. That is a nuisance, not a battle.

**Climax.** With all three panes on the shelf, they chorus without being asked. Footsteps come back late from under the water. The basins brighten. Pemma cannot hold the stack apart by herself and will not decide alone. She asks what should be done with the glass now that it has been heard. There is no enemy in the chorus. Walking away leaves the panes couched in weed so they do not touch, and the choice waits until the next visit. Dusk does not destroy them.

**Resolution.** The party chooses once. Pemma acts immediately, the chorus stops in the way that choice implies, and the quest flag closes. The Tropical Shore, the Pirate Camp, the ship, and the world map are otherwise as they were. Returning later shows only the aftermath of that choice.

## Important player choices

The quest has one deciding choice, made at the climax, after all three panes have been heard. It has three resolutions. Lesser lift-and-listen choices can be retried and do not lock this one. Refusing to help at the start is a delay, not a resolution: Pemma and the panes remain.

**What should be done with the glass?**

1. **Give the panes back to the tide.** Lower them into the basins and let the next tide carry the voices out. They are not kept, copied, or broken.
2. **Leave them with Pemma as an archive.** Separate the panes on the shelf so they can be heard one at a time and cannot chorus again unless someone stacks them.
3. **Break the panes and bury the pieces above the tide line.** End the voices. Do not drop the shards where the sea can grind them back into a lens.

The party may also keep postponing. Postponing does not pick up the Black Mask, does not call Leila's crew, and does not start a voyage.

## Possible resolutions

Each resolution completes the quest. None of them sets a story phase, moves a guest, or alters the Pirate Camp.

**Give the panes back to the tide.** Pemma lowers them. The late footsteps stop. On later visits the pools are only pools, and the voices do not return. She stays long enough to see the tide take them, then remains on the island as a quiet watcher who no longer lifts glass. She will sit with the party once: the living are restored to full HP and MP. The dead are not raised. After that single rest, sitting is only quiet. The sea also uncovers a **Coral Knife** that the green-net fisher's pane had been holding under the sand (knife, attack 18, no element, sellable, not in any shop, not a key). It is a keepsake with a modest edge, weaker than a mythril knife.

**Leave them with Pemma as an archive.** She wedges the panes apart with driftwood and writes which voice is which. The party may return any time they can dock and listen to one pane again. The chorus does not rebuild unless a later visit is used only as listening, which it is: there is no second choice and no new quest. She pays **500 gil** and gives **2 Ethers** for the lifting. She stays on the ledge for the rest of the game. The Coral Knife stays buried. The pools do not become a rest.

**Break the panes and bury the pieces.** Pemma helps, then wishes she had not had to. The voices stop at once, including the late footsteps. She leaves the island before the party returns to the ship. A later visit finds bare rock, ordinary water, and no note that points anywhere else. She leaves **200 gil** and a **Hi-Potion** from her pack. No knife, no archive, no rest. The quest is still complete.

All three are available as soon as the chorus starts. None is locked behind a fight, a keyword, or another side quest. NPCs elsewhere never mention which one was chosen.

## Gameplay opportunities

Listening is the play. Each pane is a field conversation with two or three lines to attend to. Attending is how the pane "clears" enough to set down. The lines are flavor plus one local fact (a shifted shoal depth, a tern heading, the delay in the bird cry). Missing a line does not block the climax; clouding a pane only forces a retry.

Lifting is the other verb. A three-way edge choice, success or cloud, no HP cost, no encounter check. It should feel careful, not like a reflex test the player can lose for good.

The new map is a quiet room off a dungeon that otherwise still has its forest encounter rate and its camp boss. Keeping the pools at encounter rate 0 is the point: a random fight would talk over the glass. Do not add pirates, sea monsters, or a boss to this map.

No new keyword is added to the fifteen, and `learnKeyword` is not called. The keyword menu can still be opened on Pemma; she uses the ordinary "nothing new to say about that" response.

The Coral Knife, if taken, uses the existing knife skill and the existing equip rules. Gil and Ethers and the Hi-Potion use existing items. The one-time rest on the release resolution restores the living the way an inn does, but it charges nothing, does not raise the dead, and does not use a sanctuary. It fires once, from a local flag, only after release.

Sail, Talk on the camp, and the Black Mask chest are not reused as the activity. A player who later walks into the Pirate Camp is in the main place again, not in this quest.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Tropical Island was a fishing edge before it was a convenient cove. The dead in the glass are locals who misread these shoals. Their loss is ordinary and old. It is not laid at the Emperor's feet, and it is not a rebel martyrdom.

The shoals are why a keel can kiss the bottom here and why a ship from the sky cannot see the rise. That only confirms what travel already enforces. The fisher's corrected depth is color for this beach, not a new harbor and not a landing exception for the airship.

Sea-glass on this island is storm-sand, not Mysidian work. It keeps sound that was spoken low over moving water, the way a shell does, only more faithfully and only for a while. Birds are caught when they skim the same pools. The glass cannot hold a password, a spell, or a name it was never told. Stacked panes chorus because the shore is one instrument, not because a will is inside it.

The cold face of each pane points inland, toward the camp, and the warm face points to sea. Pemma treats that as a fact about how the panes cooled. She does not investigate the camp, and the quest does not explain the pirates.

## Rewards

Rewards are optional and local. None of them is a key, a keyword, a guest, or a step on the Mysidia road.

- **Release:** Coral Knife (knife, attack 18, sellable addition, no shop) and one free full restore of HP and MP for the living at the pools. No raise, no second rest.
- **Preserve:** 500 gil, 2 Ethers, and repeatable one-pane listening on later docks. No weapon and no rest.
- **Break:** 200 gil and 1 Hi-Potion. No return visit content.

The knife is intentionally behind mythril. Missing the quest, or choosing a resolution that does not include the knife, leaves the party no weaker for the campaign.

## Canon dependencies

The party needs a ship that can dock at Tropical Island. In this game that ship is the one recovered when the Leviathan is left behind, and the airship still cannot open island harbors. That is an existing travel fact, not a flag this quest checks. The quest does not read `piratesDefeated`, `voyageStarted`, `leilaJoined`, the Leviathan phase, or the Roundworm victory, and it does not write them.

It depends on no side quest. It does not use the Gatrea dispatch, the Rebel Armband, Heron Glade, or the Alarm Clock.

It does not teach or consume Wild Rose, Mythril, Dreadnought, Airship, Sunfire, the Goddess's Bell, Dragoons, Wyverns, Mysidia, Mask, Ekmet Teloess, Cyclone, Palamecia, the Ultima Tome, or Jade Passage. It does not move Firion, Maria, Guy, Leon, Hilda, Minwu, Josef, Gordon, Leila, Ricard, Cid, Paul, Borghen, Scott, or the Emperor, and it does not kill, revive, or replace any of them.

Leila's joining, her ambush, the Leviathan, and the Roundworm remain only in the main plot, in their existing order. The Black Mask remains only in its camp chest for the main plot.

## Collision risks

- **Do not insert `tropical-pools` into the floor chain.** The dungeon builder links floors in order. A third name on Tropical Island would sit between shore and camp or beyond the camp. The pools need their own map and a single side stair from `tropical-1`, with the shore-to-camp stair left in place.
- **Do not start this from Sail.** Sail already sets `tropical-2` and begins the pirate battle. A naturalist on that map, or a stair required before the camp, would delay or replace the ambush.
- **Do not touch the pirate boss tile.** Victory over that encounter sets the Leviathan phase and moves the party into the beast. A rematch, a moved tile, or a pools exit that lands on it would replay the voyage.
- **Do not move, empty, or require the Black Mask chest** (`tropic-mask` on the Pirate Camp). Do not put a second mask, a fake mask, or a pane that satisfies the Mysidian altar in this quest.
- **Do not give Leila quest dialogue** that apologizes again, warns the party, calls her crew, or changes when she joins or leaves.
- **Keyword talk must stay inert here.** A pane that repeated Mask, Wild Rose, or Ekmet Teloess would fake a learn. Dull glass and Pemma's ordinary refusal are the safe response.
- **Do not add a ship or an airship exception.** No new landing, no canoe shortcut, no world-map harbor.
- **The one-time rest must not run the sanctuary or inn code paths** that raise the dead or charge gil, and it must not be available before the release resolution.
- **Names in the glass** must stay local fishers and birds. Using the Emperor, Minwu, Ricard, or Scott would collide with deaths and revelations that belong elsewhere.
- **Do not hang a follow-up** in Paloom, Poft, Mysidia, or on Pemma after the choice. A preserve archive is a return visit to this same ledge only.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

The whole thing happens in one pocket of Tropical Island and ends when the party decides what the glass is for. Skipping it, delaying it, or finishing it any of the three ways changes no phase, no guest, no keyword, and no chest the campaign needs. The Pirate Camp, Leila's betrayal, the Leviathan, and the Roundworm play out exactly as they do when this ledge is never found.

Pemma is not referenced by Hilda, Cid, the Mysidia elder, or anyone else. She does not offer a second errand. The voices know this shore and no farther. What the player carries away, if anything, is a minor knife, a little gil, or a place to listen again, and none of those opens a door.
