## Quest title

The Lee Fire

## Existing-world touchpoint

The fire stands on the eastern grass lip of the snowfield, outside the Snow Cavern and not on the way into it. In the current map the cavern is the snow dungeon at (18, 4). The deep white between that door and the eastern shelf is snow terrain. Foot travel does not cross snow. The snowcraft, if the party already has it, is the vehicle that does. This quest does not use that crossing.

The camp tile is the grass square (33, 7). It sits on the same southern edge as the snowfield: snow is the next square north, at (33, 6), and the grass shelf joins the eastern landmass a person can already walk from Poft, Salamand, and Bafsk. The snowcraft's boarding square stays (32, 7), one step west, and is not the camp. Heron Glade stays (22, 6). Kashuan Keep stays the grass punch at (26, 3). Nothing in this quest is placed on the Snow Cavern's tile, on its three floors, or on the snowcraft's square.

From the lip, the Snow Cavern is a dark idea to the west, across white the surveyor will not enter. She treats the cavern as a hole in the field, not as a job. The party may look that way. The quest never sends them in.

## Premise

Someone is keeping a small fire in the lee of the last trees, where grass still holds a boot and the snow begins. The tender is Maret, a surveyor who measures the edge: how far the crust will take a person's weight, which way the wind throws sparks, how many nights the wood will last. She is not lost, not a rebel courier, and not an imperial officer. The fire is a signal for anyone still out on the shelf and a risk, because a tall flame is also a road.

The work of the quest is to reach that fire from the grass, by a quiet path or by a shorter line that may involve wolves or one lost imperial scout, and then to choose whether to answer it. Answering means stepping into the light and speaking. Not answering means stopping at the light's edge and leaving on your own tracks. The deep snow, the cavern mouth, and the sled at (32, 7) are outside the job.

## Why the player becomes involved

No one assigns it. The main road's objective does not mention it. On the eastern shelf the fire is visible: a low orange mark against white silence, with cut wood stacked on the leeward side and a line of cairns along the trees. A party already walking that grass — toward Bafsk, toward the snowcraft's usual square, or simply along the lip — can close the distance or keep going. Cold is the reason to care. A tended fire at a killing edge is either a person who knows the shelf or a lure. Finding out which is practical. Ignoring it is also practical, and the game does not punish that.

If the party already rides the snowcraft, they still answer on the grass. The sled can bring them to the lip. It is not required, and it is not moved. A party that has never boarded it walks the same grass and finishes the same choice.

## Key NPCs

**Maret**, surveyor. A living woman in a hide coat, with a counting-stick, a pot of fat, and birch-bark notes. She talks in counts: nights, wind, how many steps the crust held yesterday. She does not ask to be rescued, does not join the party, and does not swear any oath. She is not Josef and not Borghen. She has no lines about a bell, a boulder, a warship, or a password. If the party mentions a sled, she says only that a sled is for the deep white, that the lip does not need one, and that the one on the shelf is not hers to move.

**An unnamed imperial scout**, optional and avoidable. One soldier, separated from a counting party, lantern almost out, not a captain and not a boss. He is not Borghen and does not become him. He does not know the party's names, does not offer to join, and does not reveal anything the main route has not already put on the objective line. If the party passes wide, he never sees them. If they fight, he is an ordinary soldier encounter and can be fled. He is not required for any resolution.

Wolves on the short line are animals, not characters. They are not a named pack and not a boss.

## New locations or spaces

**Lee Camp**, one small field map entered only from world tile (33, 7). Grass and a windbreak of cut fir, a fire pit in the lee, stacked deadwood, Maret's kit, and a cairn at the light's edge with a parcel tied for strangers. The exit returns to (33, 7). There are no stairs, no second floor, and no passage north onto the snow or west into the Snow Cavern.

The approach is chosen on the world map before entry, not as a dungeon:

- The long way follows Maret's cairns along the treeline and enters the camp from the east side of the screen.
- The short way cuts the open shelf and enters from the south side.

Encounter rate on both the camp and the long way is zero by construction, not by luck. The short way has two optional field prompts and no random table. The camp does not overwrite the snowfield's encounter list, the Snow Cavern's floors, or the world tile where the snowcraft is boarded.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** The party is on foot on the eastern grass, at the snow's edge, with the Snow Cavern still west across white they are not entering. A fire burns in the lee at (33, 7). Tracks and cairns show a person is feeding it on purpose. The snowcraft square to the west is unchanged if the sled is there; this beat does not board it. If the party walks away, nothing starts and the main objective stays as it was.

**Escalation.** Closing in means picking a way across the margin. The long way is cairns, wind, and no fight. The short way crosses wolf sign: the party may face two snow wolves or turn onto the long way before any battle. Farther along that short line, the lost scout's lantern shows. The party may pass wide, unseen, or confront him. Flee works on either fight. Both ways end at the same fire. Maret does not call out. The white stays quiet.

**Climax.** At the fire the party can see her and the stacked wood. She will look up only if spoken to. The choice is whether to answer the signal: step into the light and speak, or stop at the edge of the light and leave. There is no combat in this beat. She does not bar the exit. Answering opens two practical replies — tend the flame through the night, or bank it to coals — and then the quest is decided. Not answering decides it without a conversation.

**Resolution.** The camp settles into one of the three outcomes below. Maret lives in all of them. The Snow Cavern door, the snowcraft's square, and the main objective are the same as before the party saw the fire. A local note records the outcome so the choice is not offered again. Leaving the camp and returning later yields only a short line if she was answered, or silence if she was not. That return is not a new quest.

## Important player choices

**How to cross the margin.** Take the cairn path, which has no battle, or take the short shelf. On the short shelf, turn aside before the wolves, fight them, or flee them; then pass wide of the scout or confront him. None of these picks chooses the quest's ending. They only decide whether reaching the fire costs a fight. A party can reach the climax having fought nothing.

**Whether to answer the fire.** This is the choice that resolves the quest. It has three resolutions, repeated under Possible resolutions.

- Answer and tend. Step into the light, speak, and feed the flame with the deadwood she has already cut. The signal stays up through the night. She says plainly that a lost scout, if he still has eyes, can use it as well as a traveler can. She keeps it anyway, because the edge kills more people than one lantern will.
- Answer and bank. Step into the light, speak, and help her smother the flame to coals. The coals warm the lee and do not show on the snow. She will not relight until the next dusk, and only if the shelf is empty. Anyone still out in the white tonight has no mark to walk toward.
- Do not answer. Come near enough to see her, take nothing from her hands, and leave on your own tracks before she looks up. The fire continues as she planned. She never learns the party came.

No choice teaches a keyword, opens the Snow Cavern, moves the snowcraft, or changes who is in the party.

## Possible resolutions

**Answered, fire tended.** Maret stays at the lip and keeps the signal. The party is known to her as travelers who helped feed it, not as rebels and not by any password. She gives the lined mittens off her spare pair, two potions, and one free rest in the lee while the fire holds (the same full recovery an inn gives the living, once, no gil). The scout, if he was never fought, may still find the light later. She accepts that. The cavern to the west is unentered. The snowcraft has not moved.

**Answered, fire banked.** Maret stays, but the signal is down to coals until the next dusk at the earliest. She gives the same lined mittens and two potions, plus 150 gil she will not spend on oil she is no longer burning, a hi-potion, and an antidote. There is no free rest, because there is no flame tall enough to keep a camp by. The shelf is harder to find in the dark, which is the point. She does not ask the party to hunt the scout. The cavern and the snowcraft square are unchanged.

**Unanswered.** The party leaves without speaking. Maret keeps whatever watch she had already chosen, unwarned and unthanked. The quest is still over: the local note records that the fire was seen and not answered, and the choice is not offered again. At the cairn, which she packed for strangers who would not enter the light, the party may take one potion and 40 gil. They do not receive the mittens or a rest. If they refuse the parcel too, they leave with nothing, and that still counts as resolved. She will not hail them on a later visit.

Fighting the wolves, fighting the scout, or fighting no one can sit in front of any of the three. Those fights pay only ordinary gil and do not open, close, or upgrade a resolution. Defeating the scout does not remove him from any main-story roster, because he is not on one.

## Gameplay opportunities

The quest is a short walk and a talk, with combat only if the player picks it. The long cairn path and a wide pass around the scout are battle-free on purpose, so a party that does not want a fight is not depending on a flee roll or a low encounter rate.

The optional fights are one screen each, built from the existing battle commands. Two snow wolves, ordinary beasts, fleeable, no boss flag. The scout is a single soldier-scale encounter, also fleeable, also without a boss flag. Winning is a little gil and ordinary weapon or spell practice under the game's use-based growth. Losing is an ordinary wipe, not a scripted story loss. Neither fight changes Maret's dialogue.

The climax is a three-way field choice, not a keyword and not a menu of canon words. The tended-fire rest reuses inn recovery for the living, once, so it does not invent a new healing rule. Save stays where it already is, on the world map; the camp does not need its own save point.

If the party has the snowcraft, boarding and riding remain the existing ride actions. Dismounting on the grass lip is how a rider enters the camp. The quest must not auto-board, auto-dismount, or relocate the sled. If they do not have it, no prompt offers it here. Airship landing on this grass, much later, can also reach the tile and is not required.

The camp map should stay a small purposeful place: fire, wood, cairn, exit. It should not be an empty room that ambushes every step.

## Worldbuilding revealed

The snowfield has a lip that people still measure. East of the white, the grass is the last reliable ground. Maret's work is to walk that boundary, mark rotten crust, and keep one leeward fire so a traveler on foot can find the shelf before dark. The fire is kept low and on the grass side so sparks die in snow, not in the trees.

Foot travel is an edge practice. The deep white is a different surface. She will say so if asked, and she will not pretend a careful walker can replace a sled there. She also will not lend a sled. The machine that may already sit one square west is someone else's, found where it is found.

Wolves work the treeline, where grass, trees, and crust meet. They do not range the open white in this quest. Imperial soldiers die of weather here the same as anyone. A lost scout with a failing lantern is a counting party that came apart, not proof of a garrison at the cavern mouth. Maret has seen lanterns on the snow before. She does not know their orders, and she does not invent them.

She has no account of the Snow Cavern's inside. To her it is a darker hole west of the field, and people who walk toward it on foot stop being her concern once they leave the crust she has tested. That ignorance is deliberate. The quest does not smuggle news of what the main route is going there to do.

## Rewards

Rewards are local and modest. They are not keywords, not key items, and not canon gear.

- Tend the fire: lined mittens (hands, defense 3, a small resistance to ice — below the bronze gauntlets already sold in the east, and not a substitute for later ice gloves), two potions, and one free full rest for the living.
- Bank the fire: the same mittens and two potions, plus 150 gil, one hi-potion, and one antidote. No rest.
- Do not answer: optional cairn parcel of one potion and 40 gil. No mittens and no rest.

Wolf and scout gil, if any, is incidental battle loot and is not part of the resolution. No new spell tome. No accessory that copies an evasion band. No item that cures a status the shelf did not inflict. Suggested new ids, if implemented later: `lined-mittens`, and a local flag for the outcome. Do not reuse existing item ids.

## Canon dependencies

None. The quest does not read or set the main phase, does not check any of the fifteen keywords, and does not require the snowcraft, the Snow Cavern, Josef, or any other side quest. It becomes reachable when the party can walk the eastern grass to (33, 7), which is the same landmass as Poft once travel to that shore exists. That is ordinary geography, not a quest flag.

It stays available before the snowcraft is boarded, after it is boarded, and after the main snow errand is done. Timing does not change Maret's words. She does not react to who is alive, who is traveling with the party, or what has been taken from the cavern, because she never learns those facts. Skipping the fire changes no main-route step.

## Collision risks

- **Snow Cavern.** Do not add an exit from Lee Camp onto snow, do not move the cavern from (18, 4), and do not alter its floors, chests, or boss. The bell chest, Borghen's fight, and the boulder farewell are untouched. Maret must not mention them.
- **Snowcraft.** `SNOWCRAFT_AT` remains (32, 7). The camp trigger is only (33, 7). Standing on the sled's square must still be the existing board action and nothing else. Do not start this quest by boarding, and do not set `vehicles.snowcraft` here.
- **Foot path.** The camp tile must stay grass. A trigger on every snow tile, or a block on the grass shelf, would cut the eastern lip and the ride onto the white.
- **Nearby added and story places.** Do not use (22, 6) or the chocobo trail there. Do not use Kashuan's grass at (26, 3), Bafsk's town tile, or the mountain approach to Palamecia.
- **Objective line.** Do not call the phase setter and do not replace the on-screen objective. A side note is enough. The quest must not teach Wild Rose, Mythril, Dreadnought, Airship, Sunfire, Goddess's Bell, Dragoons, Wyverns, Mysidia, Mask, Ekmet Teloess, Cyclone, Palamecia, Ultima Tome, or Jade Passage.
- **People.** Do not kill, revive, replace, or recruit Firion, Maria, Guy, Leon, Hilda, Minwu, Josef, Gordon, Leila, Ricard, Cid, Paul, Borghen, Scott, or the Emperor. The scout is a new unnamed soldier encounter, not a renamed canon enemy and not a global edit to the soldier's stats.
- **Items and systems.** New reward ids only. Do not reuse the courier's dispatch, the rebel armband, the glade's cache, or the alarm clock, and do not mirror those quests. Inn recovery, flee, and use-based growth stay as they are.
- **Repeat entry.** After any resolution, the tile must not trap the party in the choice again or soft-lock the snowcraft square beside it.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

The Lee Fire begins and ends on one grass square at the snow's edge. Its only required action is to reach the fire and decide whether to answer. The quiet path means a party can do that without winning a battle. All three answers leave Maret where she was, leave the Snow Cavern sealed to this quest, and leave the snowcraft where the main route already finds it.

Nothing it gives is needed later. Nothing it withholds blocks the rebellion, the crossing of the white, or any other side errand. A party that never walks to (33, 7) plays the same campaign. A party that does only learns how one surveyor keeps a night on the lip, and what it costs to answer a light in that silence.
