## Quest title

The Humming Skiff.

## Existing-world touchpoint

The quest is in Poft. Poft is the eastern harbor town at world (27, 18), map id `poft`, the place the Paloom packet puts the party ashore once passage is paid. It already has the Poft Inn at 30 gil, a sanctuary, a counter selling potions, hi-potions, ethers, and cottages, and the tomes for Cure, Blink, Protect, and Shell. Cid already stands in the town hall. He is not this quest's client, and he is not its shipwright. From a new yard-shed behind the inn lane he can be seen working, small and distant, under a separate cover on the strand. Walking up to him still means leaving that shed and speaking to the Cid who is already in the hall, with only the lines he already has. The first time he speaks, he still teaches the word Airship and refuses to fly yet. Later, on the phases where he already lends the machine, he still lends it, and after that he still says she is yours. Nothing in this quest moves him, rewrites him, or hands that machine over.

## Premise

An apprentice shipwright named Lisse keeps an unfinished skiff in the Keel Shed, a tar-smelling yard-shed that opens on Poft's back lane. She is not Cid, and she was never his apprentice. The skiff was her master Ansel's last frame. He chalked the ribs, left the caulking half done, and did not come back from a night when the bay was flat. The lines almost close. They fail by about a thumb, so the sheer wants to sit a finger higher than any honest harbor boat. Whenever a storm's edge passes over Poft, even a dry thunderhead that drops no rain, the bare hull hums: three low notes, then quiet. Nets come up empty on those nights. Children sleep badly. The wood is warm along one copper nail-line when the notes sound.

The quest is the decision about that hull. Fair the unfair curve and finish an ordinary skiff. Cast the unfinished shape off empty and let the weather take it. Or put pitch to the ribs and burn the sound out. There is no enemy inside the hum, no knight, no beast, and no machine of war. The eeriness is the craft itself: a measurement that will not close, a boat that answers thunder, and a town that has to choose whether the answer is a tool, a burial, or a mistake.

## Why the player becomes involved

The party is already going to be in Poft. After Scott's ring is delivered, Hilda sends them toward Salamand, and the sailor in Paloom sells the packet across. The inn's back wall conducts the hum on the night a front goes over, and the innkeeper will name the lane if asked why the beds tick. Lisse stops travelers at the shed door because the yard itself will not decide. Mab, who mends nets, wants the ribs burned. Osric, who caulks, will not pick a side. Asking Cid is the wrong errand: Lisse will not have Ansel's oak broken up for timber under that distant cover, and she will not pretend the two works are one work.

Involvement is a choice. If the player never opens the shed, Poft keeps its inn, its shops, its priest, and Cid, and the campaign objective never mentions a skiff. If they listen and then leave, the hull simply goes on humming until they come back. No one is lost, no road closes, and no later town asks whether the shed was visited.

## Key NPCs

Lisse is the apprentice, young enough that the yard still calls her that, precise with a plane and bad at public argument. She can point to the exact rib where Ansel's chalk leaves the gap. She cannot bring herself to shave it, launch it, or burn it alone, because each act says something final about a man who is not here to correct her. She is not a party guest. She does not join, does not fight, and does not leave Poft. If the player suggests fetching the man on the strand, she refuses in one mood, every time: he is not her master, that covered work is not this hull, and she will not trade ribs for his timber. She never names his machine and never teaches a word for it.

Ansel is the absent master. He is not a face on the map, not a body in the sanctuary, and not a disguise for anyone the story already has. The quest never proves he is dead and never brings him home. Osric's memory is the only portrait: Ansel drew the water a finger high of the harbor and called the missing thumb of measurement "the weather's share." He rowed out on a windless night and the boat he took was not this one. That smaller boat is not found. Ansel stays a gap in the town, which is the point of the chalk.

Mab mends nets on the lane and speaks like a person who has counted fish, not like a mystic. She wants the hull split and burned before another night of empty nets. She will loan a hatchet and stand in the doorway for the burning. She will not swing it herself. If the player promises her ash and then does something else, she remembers, and she says so, but she does not bar the inn or the counter.

Osric is a retired caulker who sleeps on a pallet in the shed loft because the inn stairs bother his hip. He knows which seam will weep and which chalk mark is older than the others. He will brace a rope if asked and will not choose the hull's fate. He is the one who says, quietly, that most "weather's share" in Poft is only a loose fit and a story, and that this frame is the one that hummed, which is why the story has not died in this shed.

Cid remains the Cid already in Poft's hall. In this quest he is scenery at a distance and an unchanged conversation up close. He does not enter the Keel Shed, does not inspect the skiff, does not gain or lose a line, and does not alter when he lends what he lends. Before that lending, the distant cover on the strand is occupied by his work and his figure. After it, the stand is bare, and no new sentence is put in his mouth to explain the absence. Lisse, if asked then, will only say he took the covered work away and the shed is still hers to answer for.

## New locations or spaces

One new space: the Keel Shed, a single-room yard-shed on the back lane of Poft, not a new pin on the world map and not a name in the Travel list. The town map gains a door. Inside, the floor is sand and tar, the seaward end is open to the weather, and the skiff sits on short rollers under a roof that does not quite cover the bow. Rib molds lean on the west wall. A chalked board hangs over the bench. Osric's loft is a ladder and a blanket. A copper nail-line under the keel is the only thing that is warm.

The "yard" is the open end of this same room, not a second map. Rain blows in. The strand and the distant cover are a view, not a path. Leaving by the lane door returns to Poft proper, still location `poft`, so rest, save, shop, sanctuary, and the hall all behave as they do now. The shed has no encounter table, no chest of ore, and no stairs down. Ansel's board is readable scenery. It is not a key item and not one of the fifteen words.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** The player can step into this the first time they can walk Poft, which is the ferry, and on any later visit until the hull is decided. The hum is a bed-tick at the inn when a front is due, or a faint note in the lane if they poke around before sleeping. Lisse states the problem without asking them to save the town: the chalk does not close, the hull sings when storms pass, and she wants a hand that does not have to live beside Mab afterward. The player may refuse. Refusal sets nothing in the campaign and leaves her at the door. Acceptance is only an agreement to look and to come back when the sky goes the color of a coin. Cid is visible at the open end, working under his own cover if he has not yet lent his machine, or absent from that stand if he has. He cannot be hailed from here.

**Escalation.** Inside, the player reads the board, looks under the keel, and hears the two neighbors. The chalk is a triangle that fails to meet. Osric explains the weather's share and the tide hour Ansel wrote that is not on the post by the water. The nail-line ticks against a clear sky if thunder is anywhere in the region, which the player can test by waiting through one inn night or by leaving Poft and coming back: the tick starts before the clouds stand up over the shed. Mab arrives with her request to burn. The player may promise her, promise Lisse to wait for the storm, or promise neither. Promises are remembered in dialogue and do not lock the final act. No fight occurs. No item has to be carried to another town. The only preparation that matters is ordinary: a healthy party, and optionally Protect or Shell from the counter already in Poft, which will blunt the vibration later. The shed can be left freely. The storm does not start in the same breath as the agreement. It starts the next time the player sleeps at the Poft Inn after agreeing, or, if they never sleep there, the next time they re-enter the shed after having been on any other map. That keeps the beat from being missable and keeps it from being instant.

**Climax.** The storm is on the shed, not on the world map. Rain drives in the open end. The three notes are loud enough to itch in the teeth, and they are not words. The skiff strains at the stern rope as if the rollers were a slope. There is a skill check, then the decision. The check is Hold the stocks. The player chooses a living party member to brace the stern rope, or lets the rope run. A brace costs that person hit points from the vibration: 15 if their strength is under 10, 5 if their strength is 10 or more, and 1 if Protect or Shell is already on them. The loss cannot drop them below 1 hit point, cannot kill them, and cannot send the party to Game Over. The damage is ordinary harm, so the game's existing growth from taking hits may notice it, and a cast of Protect or Shell beforehand is ordinary spell use. Letting the rope run, or bracing and then releasing, marks the stocks as slipped: the hull walks one roller toward the rain and a garboard seam cracks. A second, smaller prompt hoods the lamp or opens it. Hooding keeps the pitch dark. Opening it shows the chalk jump in time with the third note, so the gap is visibly the same width as the sound. Neither lamp choice removes an ending. If the stocks held, all three fates are open. If they slipped, the wood has sprung and fairing it true is no longer offered. There is still no battle, no enemy sprite, and no victory screen. The climax ends when the player chooses the hull's fate under the rain.

**Resolution.** Morning comes in the same shed, weather ordinary, hum either gone or gone with the boat. Lisse, Mab, and Osric each have a short response that matches the fate below and whether any promise was broken. The shed remains enterable as a quiet place: a finished skiff's empty stocks, a scorch on the sand, or a bare roller, depending. The campaign objective is not rewritten. Cid, if spoken to in the hall, is still only Cid. The quest flag closes and does not open a follow-up anywhere else.

## Important player choices

The player may refuse Lisse at the door, or accept and then abandon the shed for as long as they like. That choice has no resolution of the hull. The skiff keeps humming, the main road through the story stays as it is, and coming back later still works, including after long stretches of the campaign. Promising Mab the fire, or promising Lisse to wait, is a second choice. It changes morning dialogue and changes nothing about what the rollers will physically allow.

The choice that decides the quest is made in the storm: what becomes of the hull. Fair the wood, but only while the stocks still hold, and plane the thumb-wide gap away until the sheer sits at a harbor waterline. Give the hull to the weather, casting it off empty along Ansel's chalk, which is available whether or not the brace held. Or burn the ribs with pitch and the lamp, also available either way. Give the hull to the weather is one choice with two distinct resolutions. If Hold the stocks succeeded, the unfinished skiff leaves whole and does not come back. If Hold the stocks failed and the seam has cracked, the same order to cast off sends a wounded hull onto the harbor bar, where it breaks, and the sound dies in the pieces. Fairing and burning are single resolutions of their own choices. No choice here hands anyone a vehicle, a guest, or a campaign key.

## Possible resolutions

**A true skiff.** The stocks held, and the player faired the wood. Lisse shaves Ansel's gap, the hum stops on the stroke that removes it, and they caulk an ordinary boat. On the next calm day it sits in Poft's harbor as a working skiff for the fishers. It has no name, because Lisse will not paint one. It is not added to the party's boats, does not appear in Travel, and does not cross to Paloom. Mab is satisfied that the nets will be ordinary again. Osric allows that they will never know whether Ansel meant the gap. Lisse can sleep, and she stays an apprentice who has finished one hard boat. Reward: the Caulker's Jack, and 100 gil from Lisse's jar.

**Given to the weather.** The stocks held, and the player cast the hull off along the chalk. Nobody boards it. It sits oddly high in the wash, clears the bar, and is gone in the rain. It does not return, does not beach later as a dungeon, and is never found in another sea of the campaign. The hum leaves with it. By dawn the last warm nail has worked out of the roller into Osric's hand. He gives it over. Lisse hangs the chalk board by the inn door and keeps a storm-watch for the town, which is a job, not a legend: she looks at the sky and tells Mab when to double the nets. Reward: the Weather Nail.

**Wreck on the bar.** This is the other resolution of that same cast-off choice, taken when the stocks had already slipped. The cracked garboard does not survive the bar. The hull breaks where people can see it at low water. The hum ends in the scatter of ribs. Nobody drowns, because nobody was aboard. The nail is lost in the wreckage, and the wreckage is scenery, not a salvage dungeon and not a hazard to the ferry. Lisse keeps the chalk board and does not hang it up. She blames the slide more than the player, and she is not comforted by that. Osric gives what he can. Reward: three potions. No accessory, no armor, no gil.

**Pitch and ash.** The player burns the ribs, lamp hooded or open. The notes climb one step and stop. The shed walls are smothered by Osric if a spark jumps; Poft does not catch fire, and the inn is untouched. Morning is tar-smoke and a blackened keel-line. Mab leaves two things and her thanks. Lisse will not talk until evening. Then she says she can sleep and that she hates the reason. Ansel's board stays, the chalk smeared. If the player had promised Lisse to wait and then burned the frame anyway, she adds that, once, and does not repeat it on later visits. Reward: 200 gil and one hi-potion from Mab. The mystery is ended by destruction, not by an answer.

Walking away before the storm is not one of these four. The hull is still unfinished, and the four remain possible later, except that a failed brace only exists inside the storm itself. A player who held the stocks cannot afterward pretend they slipped.

## Gameplay opportunities

The Keel Shed is a small field map linked from Poft, encounter rate zero, still inside the town for saving and for the sanctuary's reach. The storm is a short chain of field prompts, not a battle and not a scripted loss. Hold the stocks is the skill check: pick a living member, pay a capped hit-point cost that rewards strength already trained by use and rewards Protect or Shell already known, or let the rope run and accept the slipped state. Those two protective tomes are already on Poft's counter, so the check can teach their use without requiring a purchase. Anyone can brace. A weak party pays more hit points and still reaches the choice. No one dies in the shed, and a party at the end of its strength is not barred from the lane.

The hum itself can be a sparse audio sting on the Poft map when `keelResolved` is unset and the local front flag is up: three notes, no combat modifier, no encounter-rate change. After a resolution, the sting stops. Reading the board, examining the nail, and talking to Lisse, Mab, and Osric are ordinary talk actions. Promises are local flags. Suggested flags are `keelMet`, `keelWaiting`, `keelSlipped`, and `keelResolved` with the values true, weather, wreck, or ash. None of them are read by the main phase logic, and none of them should call the phase setter or replace the objective line. A log note is enough.

Use-based growth gets a small, honest hook. The brace's damage can count as harm taken. Casting Protect or Shell into the storm is casting those spells. There is no new spell, no new weapon skill, and no practice post. Rewards occupy slots the game already has: a body armor piece, or an accessory, or consumables and gil. The Weather Nail does not need a new combat rule. Its tick is a single log line the first time it is equipped, and its numbers are evasion and a point of defense. It does not quiet encounters, does not grant a ride, and does not forecast the campaign.

The distant Cid view is a backdrop that swaps on the existing lend flag: covered work and a figure before, bare stand after. It is not an interactable, not a second Cid, and not a way to start his conversation early or late.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Poft's living is still boats, tar, and nets. The shed shows the working harbor beside the hall, and it shows that not every frame on this shore belongs to the man in the hall. Apprentices here finish work their masters dropped, and the neighbors argue about it in doorways because everyone shares the same lane.

The weather's share is a local shop habit. Old molds leave a finger of slack and call it respect for the sea. Osric is clear that almost all of that is superstition and a convenient excuse for a seam that was never faired. Ansel's frame is the uncomfortable exception that keeps the habit alive: the gap hummed, the nail went warm, and the third note matched the missing thumb when the lamp was open. The quest does not promote that into a spirit, a cult, or a second school of magic. It leaves the cause as tension in the wood and as a shape that was drawn for water a finger higher than the bay. People may disagree in the morning about whether Ansel was measuring a real thing. The game does not hand down a verdict beyond what the chosen resolution does to the sound.

The hum is not speech. It teaches nothing. It is not a password, not a liturgy, and not a name for any place the party is already bound for. Poft, after the quest, is slightly more specific and no more important. Fishers either have a nameless skiff, a woman watching the sky with a chalk board, a broken hull on the bar, or a quiet shed that smells of pitch. The wider war does not move.

## Rewards

All rewards are local and optional. None of them are keys, none of them are rides, and none of them are sold from a shop afterward.

Fairing the wood gives the Caulker's Jack and 100 gil. The jack is body armor, defense 7, evasion 2, unsellable price marked at nothing, sellable if the player later dumps it. It sits between leather and the copper cuirass already associated with this stretch of the journey: a little less defense than the cuirass, a little evasion the cuirass does not have. It is a sidegrade, not a reason to skip the counter.

Giving the hull to the weather, stocks held, gives the Weather Nail and no gil. The nail is an accessory, defense 1, evasion 4. That evasion is deliberately below the Rebel Armband's, and it uses the same single accessory slot, so it competes with that band instead of stacking into a new tier. The first time it is equipped it logs that it ticks once and then stays ordinary metal.

The wreck on the bar gives three potions from Osric. Burning the ribs gives 200 gil and one hi-potion from Mab. Those consumables are the same medicines Poft already sells.

No resolution grants experience of a kind the game does not use, a permanent stat, a spell, a tome, a guest, or a map. The harbor skiff in the true-skiff ending is town scenery. It must not be implemented as a canoe, a ship, or any other entry in the vehicle flags.

## Canon dependencies

The quest depends on Poft being a town the player can enter, and on nothing else. The earliest door is the existing ferry: after the ring is reported, passage from Paloom costs 50 gil, and the boat runs to Poft. Later, the player may also walk in from the eastern shore or land by means they already earned. The shed does not care which, and it does not require the later machine from the hall. It does not read guest membership, ore, bells, masks, or any phase name. It can be finished the hour the party first arrives, or ignored until the last town they bother to revisit, or ignored forever.

Cid's dependency is negative. His presence, his teaching of Airship, his refusal, the phases on which he lends, and his line once the lending has happened stay in their current order and wording. This document does not schedule that lending and does not describe the player taking his machine. Ansel, Lisse, Mab, and Osric are new. They do not replace a death, a rescue, a joining, or a betrayal anywhere else. The main plot remains true if the Keel Shed is never built into the game at all.

## Collision risks

The highest risk is Cid. Implementers must not add branches under his talk, must not set the airship vehicle flag from the shed, and must not move his hall tile. The strand cover is a picture that follows the lend flag. It must not become a second interactive Cid with softer terms. Lisse must not teach Airship, must not repeat his refusal, and must not describe guns, a warship, or a fire that unmakes one. Those subjects are outside this shed on purpose. If a writer puts them in her mouth, the quest stops being self-contained and starts rewriting his scene.

The second risk is vehicles. A finished skiff, a vanished skiff, and a wreck on the bar are all easy to "promote" into travel. They must not touch the Paloom ferry edge, Josef's canoe, the ship that comes back from the voyage, the snowcraft, the chocobo, or the airship, and they must not add a world-map location. The Keel Shed stays off the Travel list and keeps Poft's location id so arrival and saving do not grow a new rule.

The third risk is the fifteen words and the campaign's set pieces. The hum is not a sixteenth keyword and not a redefinition of any existing one. The storm is not the Cyclone, the empty launch is not the voyage that meets the Leviathan, and the missing master is not a hidden return of anyone who dies or disappears in the main tale. Do not put a boss in Poft, do not wipe the party, and do not leave a body the sanctuary is then asked to judge. Ansel's absence is permanent ambiguity, not a death flag.

The fourth risk is other optional material. This is not a delivery to another town, not an armband, not a forest cache, and not a consumable that cures sleep. Rewards must stay weaker than or beside early shop armor, and the nail must stay a lesser accessory than the armband so the two optional gifts do not invent a combined power curve. Do not key the shed off any other side quest's flags, and do not let any other side quest key off `keelResolved`.

Dialogue collision is smaller but real. Innkeeper, shop, and priest lines can gain at most a pointer to the lane while the quest is open, and must drop that pointer afterward without changing prices or services. Party members do not need special reactions. Guest-specific lines about other boats would tangle this hull with plot vessels, so nobody in the party delivers a unique speech about it.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

Everything that changes is inside one shed in Poft and in the player's pack. Starting, abandoning, or finishing the quest does not set a story phase, does not rewrite the objective, does not move a guest, does not kill or revive anyone, and does not teach or spend a keyword. The four endings alter Lisse's work, Mab's nets, Osric's pallet, and a handful of local rewards. They do not alter who holds Fynn, who is missing, who dies, or what the party rides.

If the player never speaks to Lisse, the campaign is the campaign already written: the packet, the hall, the road onward. The hum can be omitted entirely in that case, or left as a sound with no door, and still no system downstream is waiting on it. If the player resolves the hull, no later scene checks the resolution. Cid's machine comes and goes by its own rule, whether the skiff was faired, launched, broken, or burned. The oath the party is already under does not run through this lane.
