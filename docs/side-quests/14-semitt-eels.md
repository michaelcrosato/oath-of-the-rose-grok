## Quest title

The Second Mouth

## Existing-world touchpoint

Semitt Falls, the dungeon at world (21, 12), entered in the main plot as `semitt-1`. The grass under that tile is a two-by-three shelf, `(21–22, 11–13)`, with the canoe river touching it at `(23, 12)` and running north–south on `x = 24` before it turns east along `y = 15` toward the canoe at `(32, 15)` and Salamand. Josef’s canoe is what puts the party on that bank. The mine mouth, the stairs down through Semitt Falls B1, B2, and the Depths, and everything those floors already contain stay the main plot.

This quest uses only the water above that mouth. The Upper Pools are a separate map on the same world tile. They do not link to `semitt-1`, `semitt-2`, or `semitt-3`.

## Premise

Before the river drops into the imperial dig, it stalls in three black pools. Eel-fishers work them from the shelf. They are not rebels, not soldiers, and not a fourth member of anyone’s party. They haul on lines, and they haul in song.

The song is a work song with two mouths. One voice calls the question from the pool. A different voice has to answer, or the haul does not come. The fishers say the water can tell when the same mouth does both parts, and that it takes that as mockery. Their answerer, Old Catt, has stopped singing. The pool has started finishing her sentences a heartbeat late, in a thicker voice. She will not give it her mouth again.

What answering calls is not a spell and not a god. The fishers will not name it until it has already come. If the answer is the true work verse, a long eel rolls up, takes the hook, and is already still when the line clears. If the answer is bent into a demand — including a demand for the bright metal the mines are only rumored to hold — the same presence rises wrong, and it can be fought. If no one answers, the fishers cut the line, the water stays shut, and the day’s work ends badly but ends.

## Why the player becomes involved

The party is already coming to Semitt Falls. The fishers are standing in the shallows at the top of the drop, on the way to the mine mouth and easy to walk past. A line is snagged. Hedd calls the question across the pool and leaves a silence where the answer should be. He asks the strangers on the bank to be the second mouth, because a stranger’s mouth is clean and Catt’s is not.

They do not know Wild Rose, they do not ask for help against the empire, and they do not bar the stairs. The main objective stays the main objective. The shelf is a local offer: sing the answer, sing the wrong answer, or refuse. Leaving the map without choosing does not finish the quest. Saying no does.

## Key NPCs

**Hedd**, the caller. Waist-deep in the middle pool. He sings only the question: “Black pool, cold wrist — who keeps the haul?” He will hold that line open as long as the party is on the shelf. He wants the haul finished. He does not want a war, a password, or a companion.

**Bran**, the hauler. On the bank with the snagged line over his shoulder. He explains the rule without poetry: different mouths, or the pool thinks it is being played with. He offers the clean refusal himself — don’t sing, and they cut the line. He will not be the answer. He will not go down the mine stairs.

**Old Catt**, the answerer who stopped. Alive, dry-footed, sitting on the drying poles. She hums the shape of the answer and, if the party stands through it, the words: “Wet to the wrist. Haul when it answers.” She will not sing them into the pool. She is not a prisoner, not a ghost, and not someone to rescue. If spoken to about the delayed echo, she says the water started keeping the last word, and that is enough reason to keep her mouth off it.

None of the three join the party, follow the party into the mine, or appear anywhere else. They have no rank in the rebellion and no rank in the empire. Soldiers use the wet stairs below and, so long as the lines stay out of the race, do not come up. The fishers sell eels to whoever stands on the bank with coin. They do not consider that a side.

## New locations or spaces

**The Upper Pools** (`semitt-pools`), one open shelf map, location id still `semitt-falls`. Reached from the world tile by a fork that leaves the existing entry alone: “Mine mouth” still opens `semitt-1`; “Pools above” opens this map. No stairs between them.

The map is a single wet room, not a dungeon corridor. Three pools. The near one is shallow and walkable; that shallows tile is where the answer is given. The middle pool is black and blocked; Hedd stands at its edge. The far one is under the lip of the drop, audible but not a way down. Drying poles and eel-baskets sit on the grass at the back. No random encounters. The only fight is the scripted one, and only if the song is answered wrong or the calm haul is spoiled.

Nothing on this map is the mythril vault, a cell, a sergeant’s post, or a chest of ore.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** The party reaches Semitt Falls by canoe, or later by airship landing on the shelf grass. At the world tile they can choose the mine mouth or the pools above. On the pools, Hedd is already singing the question and waiting. Bran has a line that will not come home. Catt is silent until spoken to. Hedd asks the party to step into the shallows and be the second mouth. He also tells them they may refuse. The mine remains enterable the whole time. No story phase changes.

**Escalation.** The party can talk the shelf without committing. Bran states the two-mouth rule and the refusal. Catt, if heard twice, gives the true answer up — first as a hum, then as the words, stored as a local flag, not as one of the fifteen keywords. While she hums, the pool gives the last word back late, thicker than her voice. That echo is the unease: something under the skin of the water is keeping time. Bran says the echo has been worse since the dig started pumping, and that the eels taste of ash and coin. If the party mentions the bright metal people say is in the rock, all three answer the same way: metal is not a fish, and asking the pool for it is how a haul goes wrong. They do not confirm that the metal is there. They will not fetch it.

The true answer cannot be given until Catt has been heard out. Refusal and a bent answer can be chosen as soon as Hedd has asked.

**Climax.** On the shallows tile, with the question held open, the party chooses once. Give the true work verse. Give a demand in its place (“Wet to the wrist. Haul up what they bury.”). Or decline to sing and step out of the water.

A true answer flattens the pool. A dark back, longer than Bran’s skiff, rolls once. The snagged line comes free with a great eel on it, already dead. The wash leaves a river-stone with a hole worn through it, strung on eel-skin. Striking the water after that spoils the calm and becomes the fight below; the stone is not left.

A demand swells the pool. The thing the song calls rises as an enemy — the Listener, a shelf eel, not a serpent from the sea road and not the great one that swallows ships. It is water-aspected and weak to bolt, about the weight of a hard mine fight and no harder than the one the depths already hold. It can poison on a bite. It can be fought or fled. It does not block the way out.

A refusal is the end of the singing. Bran cuts the line. The pool stays shut. Nobody follows.

**Resolution.** Whichever choice was taken, one local settled flag is set, with a variant for how. The fishers do not offer the song again. On a later visit they have one line matching the variant — the haul was clean, the haul was spoiled, or the line was cut — and then they are only fishers. The mine mouth, the floors below, and the main objective are as they were.

## Important player choices

The one choice is how to answer the haul song, made in the shallows after Hedd has called the question.

- **Answer truly**, available only after Catt’s verse is learned. The Listener comes as a finished haul, not a battle.
- **Answer with a demand**, available immediately. The verse is bent toward whatever the mines are rumored to hold. The Listener comes angry. Fighting it or fleeing it both finish the quest.
- **Decline to sing**, available immediately. The party steps out. The fishers cut the line and end the day. The quest is finished, not paused.

Walking off the map, entering the mine, or coming back tomorrow without having chosen leaves the question open. Only the three answers above settle it. Attacking the water after a true answer is not a separate quest choice; it spoils that resolution and turns it into the fight.

## Possible resolutions

**Quiet haul.** The true verse was sung and the water was not struck. The eel is landed already still. The fishers keep the meat and pay a share. The party keeps the Listener’s Stone. Catt looks at the pool as if she might someday sing again, and does not. Settled.

**Spoiled haul.** A demand was sung, or the calm eel was attacked. The Listener rises and is fought. If it is killed, it sinks, Bran’s line comes back torn, and the party may take the barbed gig off the wreck of the tackle. The fishers pay nothing and will not touch the meat. If the party flees, the Listener sinks anyway, the lines snap, and nothing is left on the bank. Either way the song has been answered, the presence does not return, and the quest is settled. No ore comes up with it.

**Cut line.** The party declines to sing. Bran cuts the snag. Hedd stops the question mid-word. Catt nods, as if this were the sensible month for it. They give the party a string of smoked eel from an earlier, ordinary day, because hands that held a line and then let it go are still owed the smell of the work. The pool does not open. Settled.

All three variants set the same settled flag. None of them frees a prisoner, opens a vault, starts a boss on the depths floor, puts mythril in the inventory, or adds a person to the party.

## Gameplay opportunities

The shelf is a field conversation and, only on the spoiled path, one optional battle. It does not add a keyword, a story phase, or a dungeon floor.

Learning the verse works like listening, not like a puzzle with a lock. Two talks with Catt record a local flag. Until that flag is set, the true answer is not on the menu; demand and refusal are. The verse never appears in the fifteen-word list, and the fishers have nothing to say to those words except the rumor note about metal.

The Listener, if fought, is one front-rank enemy: on the order of 180 HP, attack near a mid mine brute, water element, weak to bolt, a bite that can poison. Antidotes are already sold in Salamand. The encounter is fleeable, is not a scripted loss, and is not marked to a story phase or to a mine map. Use-based growth applies as in any other fight. Fleeing still settles the spoiled variant.

The shallows tile is the only place the choice appears, so the rest of the room is safe to talk in. The map has no random encounters and no chests. The fork on the world tile is the whole navigation change: the existing mine entry stays bound to `semitt-1`.

Guy may remark that the delayed echo sounds like speech. That is a line of color. He is not required, the fishers do not understand him as their singer, and nothing about him changes.

## Worldbuilding revealed

The falls have a working life above the dig. The pools are fished on a rule older than the mine: a haul song in two mouths, question from the water, answer from the bank, never both from the same throat. The fishers believe the pool can hear mockery, and the late echo is why they believe it today. They do not worship the thing that answers. They treat it as a risk of the job, the way a snag is a risk of the job.

They know the empire only as the people on the stairs and the pumps that changed the taste of the eels. They have heard the rumor that the rock under them holds a bright metal. They do not know it, do not want it, and do not haul rock. Asking the water for that rumor is a spoiled verse, and the water still does not give up ore.

They are civilians who sell fish to any buyer on the bank. That compromise is theirs. It does not make them informants, allies, or a back door into the mine.

## Rewards

Rewards are early-mine modest. They are not arms forged from ore, not key items, and not sold from any town rack.

- **Quiet haul:** the Listener’s Stone, an accessory with a small magic bonus (about +2) and no evasion trick to it, plus 120 gil as the fisher’s share of the eel. The fishers keep the catch.
- **Spoiled haul, Listener killed:** a Barbed Gig, a spear around attack 12, between a javelin and a common spear, taken from the torn tackle, plus whatever gil the fight drops. No stone. No pay from the fishers.
- **Spoiled haul, fled:** no gig, no stone, no pay. A poison bite, if it landed, is cured the ordinary way. The quest is still over.
- **Cut line:** three Smoked Eels, a consumable that restores about 40 HP and cures nothing. Less than a potion. Not a clock, not a status item.

No resolution grants mythril, a pass, a ring, a mask, a bell, or a seat in the party.

## Canon dependencies

The only dependency is geographic. The party must be able to stand on Semitt Falls. In the main route that happens when Josef’s canoe is on the river; an airship can land on the same grass later. The quest does not read or set the phase, and it does not check the depths: not the sergeant, not the cell, not the lock, not the ore.

It teaches none of the fifteen keywords and requires none of them to be asked on the shelf. Mythril is only a rumor the fishers can brush off. Choosing the demand does not yield the metal and does not count as taking it.

No locked story event is a prerequisite or a consequence. The opening loss, Leon, Wild Rose, Scott, Minwu, the Semitt rescue, Josef, the bell, Sunfire, the Dreadnought, Leila, Leviathan, the Lamia, Hilda, Fynn, Ricard, the Cyclone, Ultima, the Emperor, and the Dark Emperor play out as they already do. No named hero, guest, villain, or monarch is killed, revived, or replaced. The fishers are new, and they stay on the shelf.

There is no dependency on any other side quest.

## Collision risks

- The world tile’s entry is `semitt-1`. The pools have to be a second choice beside that entry, not a replacement, or the rescue becomes missable.
- Do not put Hedd, Bran, or Catt on B1, B2, or the Depths, where Nelly, Paul, and the Sergeant already stand.
- Do not set or require the flags that open the vault, free Nelly, mark the sergeant beaten, or forge the ore. Do not place a mythril chest, or any chest, on the shelf.
- Do not change story phase. The objective line on the way to the falls still points at the mine.
- The Listener must not be Leviathan, a sea-serpent boss, a lamia, or a new floor boss inside `semitt-3`. Killer fish and sea serpents already exist on other tables; this fight is its own encounter, used only here, and it does not join those tables.
- Do not add the haul verse to the keyword list, and do not reuse Wild Rose, Ekmet Teloess, or any other canon word as the answer.
- Do not copy the Gatrea courier, the Rebel Armband, Heron Glade, or the Alarm Clock. The stone is a small magic accessory, not an evasion armband. Smoked eel is food, not a status cure.
- Rewards must stay below the mythril arms the smith makes from the main-plot ore, and below unique late weapons.
- The fishers must not join, must not carry a message to Altair, Salamand, Bafsk, or Fynn, and must not show up in those towns after the quest.
- Guy’s optional line about the echo must not become a required animal-speech quest or a change to who he is.
- Clearing the mine first, or never clearing it, must leave this quest’s map and endings the same. Finishing this quest must leave the mine the same.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

The whole thing lives on one new map that does not connect to the mine floors. Three local people exist only there. One local flag says the verse has been heard; one settled flag, with a variant, says the song is over. Every variant — quiet haul, spoiled haul, cut line — sets that settled flag and then stops.

Nothing in the quest reads another side quest, writes another side quest, changes travel, or gates the canoe, the stairs, the smith, or any later chapter. Refusal is a real ending, not a delay. The fishers go back to their lines and do not follow. The main plot under the drop still happens only when the player walks down into it.
