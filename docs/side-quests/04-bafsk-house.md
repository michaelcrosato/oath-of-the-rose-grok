## Quest title

The House That Knocks

## Existing-world touchpoint

Bafsk. The town is already on the world map (`bafsk` at x 35, y 10, entry map `bafsk`), east of Salamand, and the town itself is not sealed. The map is the shared 16 by 12 service plan: exit at (1, 10), spawn at (2, 9), innkeeper at (3, 2), shopkeeper at (6, 2), priest at (9, 2). The Bafsk Inn still costs 40 gil. The counter still sells longswords, spears, longbows, silver cuirasses, bronze shields, Basuna, Silence, Fear, Poison, potions, hi-potions, echo screens, and Bacchus' wine. None of those conversations change.

The quest sits on empty floor in the southeast of that same map, clear of the square at (8, 4) and the hall at (4, 6). It adds no world-map pin. Nothing east of the town gate is part of this house.

## Premise

A house in Bafsk was bricked shut years ago. Door, windows, and the cap of its private well are all in the wall. From inside, something knocks: three, a pause, three again, regular as a mason's count. The gap left for air is too small for a hand. Neighbors put porridge out at dusk. By morning the bowl is back on the stoop, licked clean.

The town does not talk about it. People are more willing to repeat a rumor — that the empire is building something nearby — than to look at the bricks. That rumor is only a rumor. No one in this quest knows what is being built, no one will walk the party toward it, and asking again gets the same refusal. The house is the older fear, and it is the only fear this quest can settle.

## Why the player becomes involved

No rebel sends them. The knock is audible from the stoop while they are in Bafsk for the inn, the shop, or the road. Merta Vell, who still sets the bowl, tells them the rule: do not teach it a new count. Knocking three is only the old pattern. Knocking any other number makes the house answer with that number instead.

If the party does teach it a new count, Merta treats them as the people who have to put the count back. If they never knock, they can still inspect the bowl, the chalk, and the mortar, and the climax stays closed until they have heard a copied count that is not three. Leaving town before that point changes nothing. The main objective line is not replaced, and the road out of Bafsk stays open.

## Key NPCs

- **Merta Vell** stands at (12, 9), just off the stoop. She is the sister of Anson Vell, the man who went down the house-well and did not come up. She sells nothing off the town counter. She will give one loaf of salt-bread, already baked for tonight's bowl, if the party chooses a resolution that uses it. She never enters the house. She will not say whether the knock is her brother.
- **Kett**, a mason, stands at (14, 7). He laid the original courses with salt, ash, and clay from this yard. He still carries the trowel. He will loosen one brick only after the party has proved the knock is a copy. He will set that brick back, or lay a thicker course over it. He will not swing the hammer that breaks the wall, and he will not follow anyone underground.
- **Lark**, a neighbor's child, chalks at (11, 8). Lark only counts. Groups of three mean the old pattern. Any other group means someone answered wrong. Lark is sent indoors before any fight and is unhurt in every ending.
- **The Dry Guest** is the local thing inside the house. It is not a speaker. If the wall is broken it can be fought, once. It is a dryness that learned a person's knock and is a beat late when it tries to be that person. It is not confirmed to be Anson, and Anson never appears.

## New locations or spaces

- **The bricked face**, on the existing Bafsk map at (13, 8). A closed front, a stoop, an offering bowl, chalk marks, and one gap. This is where almost all of the investigation happens. It is an inspection point, not a dungeon entrance.
- **The loosened course.** After the copy is proved, Kett works one brick free. The gap is wider. The party still cannot step through. A second knock becomes audible under the first, a beat late, from the capped well under the floor. The cap is scenery. It is not a stair.
- **Vell House**, map id `vell-house`, one room, encounter rate 0, `locationId` still `bafsk` so it never becomes its own place on the world map. The stair from (13, 8) exists only after the party chooses to break the course and only stays if they win. The room is a hearth, a sealed well-cap, and the Guest. There is no second floor and no link to any other map.

## Quest progression / major beats

**Beginning.** On the Bafsk town map the party hears three knocks, a pause, and three more from the bricked face at (13, 8). Merta states the rule and will not hire them. Lark's chalk already shows the old groups of three. The bowl on the stoop is clean in a way a porridge bowl is not. Kett, if spoken to now, says the wall is doing its job and will not lend the trowel. The empire rumor can be heard once, from Merta, and has no second step.

**Escalation.** Investigation is the work, all of it on these tiles. Listening gives the count. The bowl's smell is dry well, not a grave, and Merta says she filled it last night and found it outside this morning. Lark says a fourth chalk mark appeared on a day when no child had knocked, and that the house should not be taught numbers. The bricked face offers a one-off choice of knocking one, two, three, or four times. The house returns the same number. Three proves nothing, because that is its own habit. One, two, or four proves it copies and does not ask. Kett, told of that answer, loosens a single brick and stops. Through the gap the late second knock can be heard from the well-cap. A nail beside the door holds Anson's old measure-cord, salted so a count would stay a count. Nobody goes inside during this beat.

**Climax.** The choice at the loosened course. Merta has the salt-bread in her hands. Kett has mortar. The brick is loose, and the wall is otherwise whole. The party chooses once, in conversation at (13, 8), among the four resolutions below. Walking away before choosing leaves the brick loose and the quest unfinished. Fleeing a fight, or falling in it, also returns to this choice. The course is not rubble unless the fight is won.

**Resolution.** The chosen resolution plays out, the quest flag closes, and Bafsk's services are as they were. The house either goes quiet, keeps the old knock, or hides the knock behind new brick. Nothing under the town is opened. The rumor about the empire is still a rumor.

## Important player choices

Lesser acts do not resolve the quest. Knocking a test count only proves the copy. Leaving Bafsk before the climax leaves the house as it was. Refusing the trowel keeps the climax shut.

**The choice at the loosened course** is the one choice that ends the quest. It has four resolutions:

- **Break and fight.** The party breaks the loosened course themselves. Kett steps back. Merta sends Lark indoors. The stair to Vell House appears, and the Dry Guest is fought there.
- **Feed it.** Merta's salt-bread is pushed through the gap as a meal. The party does not enter. Kett sets the one brick back and does not thicken the wall.
- **Wall it up.** No bread is given. Kett lays a new salted course over the gap and the loose brick, thicker than the first wall. The party does not enter.
- **Leave an offering.** The same loaf is set in the outside bowl. Someone speaks a courtesy at the gap that does not promise to open the house, to bring anyone out, or to give the knock a name. The loose brick is left as Kett set it. No new course is laid.

## Possible resolutions

**The choice at the loosened course** resolves in one of these four ways, and only one of them is taken.

- **Break and fight.** One ordinary battle against the Dry Guest inside Vell House. Victory ends the knock, because nothing is left there that knocks. The well-cap stays sealed. A hearth cache holds the combat reward. Merta sweeps the stoop, closes her stall, and will not say what was killed. She does not give up the measure-cord. If the party flees, or loses, the stair goes away and this same choice is offered again. The Guest is not a boss on a story map, and it is not fought twice.
- **Feed it.** Both knocks stop. The late knock from the well catches up to the first, and then there is silence. The brick is reset. The bowl stays out and stays empty. Merta gives the Salt Cord from the nail. She is not grateful in any bright way. The thing is still inside, fed, and this quest does not wake it again.
- **Wall it up.** The knock continues behind the new course and cannot be heard from the street. Lark's chalk is wiped, and Lark is told to count somewhere else. Merta is relieved and ashamed. Kett gives the Salt Cord; he had meant to bed the last length of it in the new mortar and does not need it. The hunger is not fed and not seen.
- **Leave an offering.** The bowl is taken in the night and set back empty, as it always was. The knock returns to three and three and stays there. Lark says that is the safe pattern. Merta gives the Salt Cord because the count was kept. The house remains a small, steady horror. Standing on the stoop still lets the party hear it. They cannot open a second climax.

## Gameplay opportunities

The central play is investigation on the Bafsk town map, using the same adjacency and Talk pattern as other field NPCs. No new keyword is learned, and none of the fifteen keywords is required. The knock test is a four-way reply on the bricked-face conversation, not a new field command and not a puzzle that leaves town.

The optional fight is a single foe, id `dry-guest`, name Dry Guest: about 130 HP, attack near 18, defense near 6, magic defense near 8, agility near 7, evasion near 2, front rank, tag `beast`. It is weak to fire because it is dry. It is not undead, so a later holy item cannot "solve" whether it was Anson. It may cast Silence, which Echo Screens from the Bafsk counter already answer; that preparation is optional. The encounter awards no gear and no meaningful gil of its own. Ordinary flee rules apply, and flee does not complete the quest. Winning is the only combat result that does. Use of weapons and spells in that battle trains ranks as any other fight. The non-combat resolutions do not fake a battle.

Salt-bread and salt-mortar are not inventory systems to manage across the world. The loaf is already in Merta's hands. The mortar is already in Kett's bucket. Neither is a battle consumable, a status cure, or a key item in the story chest. Suggested quest flags, unused by the main plot: `houseCopyProved`, `houseCourseLoose`, `houseResolved` with values `fought`, `fed`, `walled`, or `offered`.

## Worldbuilding revealed

Bafsk used to count masonry in threes. Private house-wells were normal here. When the Vell well went dry, Anson tapped the pipe to see if water would answer, and something in the shaft learned the tap. He went down. Merta and Kett bricked the door, the windows, and the cap from the outside. Salt in the mortar is a yard practice against a hunger that copies sounds. It is not a school of magic, and it is not a word of power.

The common talk of the town, that the empire is building something nearby, is revealed only as talk. Soldiers buy food. Someone asked Kett for bricks, and he had none to sell because these were already in a wall. Lark has been told not to follow carts. None of the three has seen the work, and the quest never shows it. People speak of that rumor more easily than they speak of the house. Settling the house does not make them any more willing to explain the rumor.

## Rewards

Peaceful resolutions give one permanent item. **Feed it**, **Wall it up**, and **Leave an offering** each give the Salt Cord (`salt-cord`): an accessory, evasion 3, not sold in any shop, sellable for a modest purse (about 200 gil). Accessory defense and magic defense are not the reward, because battle math does not add those from the accessory slot. The cord is Anson's measure-cord. Only one ending can give it, so it cannot be farmed. It does not mark the wearer as a rebel and does not cure a status.

**Break and fight** does not give the cord. The hearth cache (`vell-hearth`) holds 400 gil and one Hi-Potion. That is a one-time purse, in the range of a longsword, not a new weapon or armor tier. The stall closes, which removes no town service. No resolution changes inn prices, shop stock, the priest's fee, story phase, or the objective line.

## Canon dependencies

The quest needs only that Bafsk can be entered, which it already can, with no story flag. It does not read or write phase, keywords, guests, or key items. It does not depend on the opening loss, on anyone's survival, or on any later campaign event. It can be finished the first time the party reaches Bafsk, or after they have already come and gone, and either timing is valid. Completing it, abandoning it, or never starting it leaves the rebellion story on whatever beat it was already on.

## Collision risks

- Banned from this quest's fiction, flags, maps, and rewards: the Dreadnought, the pass, Paul, Bafsk Cave, the miner who shows the berth, and mythril. They already belong to Bafsk's main plot and stay there. The empire rumor has no follow-up flag and no road.
- Do not edit the miner at the square (8, 4), do not stand on that tile, and do not repeat or extend his lines. Do not use the hall tile (4, 6), where Paul stands during some phases.
- Do not connect `vell-house` by stairs, locks, or exits to `bafsk-cave-1`, `bafsk-cave-2`, or the world pin at (36, 10). The well-cap is not a dungeon. Chest ids `bafsk-longsword` and `bafsk-bow` stay where they are.
- In-fiction lines must not name the Dreadnought, mythril, Sunfire, or the berth. Those words appear in this draft only as exclusions.
- Do not add a sixteenth keyword. Do not call `setPhase`. Do not rewrite the on-screen objective.
- Do not destroy Bafsk, empty it, or gate the house on the warship having been seen. The source campaign ruins this town when that ship lifts. This project does not. This quest must not be the thing that ruins it, and it must not become missable by tying itself to that launch.
- Keep off the inn, shop, and priest tiles and off their generic lines. Do not discount the inn or add a sanctuary exception.
- Do not copy the Gatrea courier errand, the Sealed Dispatch, the Rebel Armband, Heron Glade, or the Alarm Clock. There is no delivery to another town, no rebel token, no chocobo trail, and no new sleep cure. The Salt Cord is evasion 3, not an evasion-8 badge. Salt-bread is not a consumable.
- New ids should stay specific (`merta-vell`, `kett-mason`, `lark-chalk`, `bricked-face`, `dry-guest`, `salt-cord`, `vell-house`, `vell-hearth`, and the `house*` flags) so they do not collide with story flags or with other side quests.
- Do not kill, revive, or replace any campaign character. Anson is never added to the fallen, and the Dry Guest is not his confirmed corpse. Lark is never harmed.

## Why the quest remains self-contained

Everything starts on the Bafsk town map and ends on the Bafsk town map. The only new room is a one-fight interior that shares Bafsk's location id and is unreachable unless this quest's own choice opens it. No other side quest's NPC, item, flag, or map is read or written. No report is carried to a castle, a princess, or a resistance. A party that never walks to the southeast tiles never hears a demand, and a party that walks away before the loosened-course choice is not punished. However the choice ends, the shops still sell, the inn still sleeps the living, the priest still raises the ordinary dead, and the road out of Bafsk still leads wherever the campaign was already going.
